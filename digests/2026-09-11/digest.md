### [Deimplementation of Inappropriate Feeding Practices in Early Care and Education: a Hybrid Type 3 Cluster-Randomized Trial](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-026-01537-9)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Massey-Swindle T et al. · Implement Sci (2026) · RCT (registered, not yet reporting results)

- Hybrid Type 3 cluster-RCT (n = 88 ECE sites) in Arkansas and Louisiana; sites randomized 1:1 to WISE Words intervention vs. usual practice (waitlist design).
- Target behaviors: pressuring children to eat, rushing mealtimes, offering food as reward — practices linked to food aversions, impaired self-regulation, and obesity risk in preschool-aged children consuming up to 500 meals/year in ECE settings.
- WISE Words package comprises six strategies: dynamic training using improvisation methods, peer learning collaboratives with goal setting, external facilitation, audit and feedback, environmental reminders, and tailored educational materials.
- Primary outcome: de-adoption of inappropriate feeding practices measured via direct mealtime observation (Table Talk instrument) at 12 and 24 months post-intervention.
- Child-level outcomes include BMI, skin carotenoid levels (Veggie Meter), observed willingness to try new foods, and teacher/caregiver-reported food neophobia; mechanistic pathway tests whether trust mediates strategy effects (Implementation Trust Building Theory of Change). NCT07101321.

### [The Mediating Effects of Home Environment on the Relationship Between Maternal BMI and Childhood Obesity: Findings from the ECHO-Wide Cohort](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-07368-6)
⭐⭐⭐ · Haridas DN et al. · Eur J Pediatr (2026) · n = 1,341 mother-child pairs · retrospective cohort

- Retrospective cohort using ECHO program data (USA); data collected September 2016–March 2023; children born 2002–2011, aged 3–6 years at assessment.
- Analytic sample: 1,341 mother-child pairs; exposure = maternal pre-pregnancy BMI (normal vs. overweight vs. obese); outcome = child BMI z-score in early childhood.
- Home environment assessed via Infant-Toddler HOME (IT-HOME) questionnaire across six subscales (Parental Responsivity, Acceptance, Learning Materials, Organisation, Variety, Parental Involvement); structural equation modelling used to test mediation.
- Children of overweight and obese mothers had significantly higher BMI z-scores compared to children of normal-weight mothers across all six subscale models and total IT-HOME z-score model.
- Home environment did not mediate the maternal BMI → child BMI z-score association; authors flag dietary quality and physical activity stimulation as candidate mediators for future investigation.

### [D-Amino Acid Prodrug DLMEH Activates mTORC1 via Sestrin2 to Restore Muscle Protein Synthesis in Sarcopenia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.09.007)
⭐⭐⭐ · Shim JH et al. · Mol Ther (2026) · preclinical (human myotubes + rat model)

- D-leucine methyl ester hydrochloride (DLMEH) is a metabolically stabilized prodrug of leucine incorporating D-stereoisomer conversion, methyl esterification, and hydrochloride salt formation to improve stability and bioavailability vs. L-leucine.
- Binding affinity to Sestrin2: Kd = 28.3 μM (three orthogonal biophysical methods), equivalent to L-leucine; Sestrin2-dependent mTORC1 activation confirmed via siRNA knockdown and rapamycin co-treatment.
- In human primary myotubes: DLMEH 100 μM restored dexamethasone-suppressed protein synthesis by 58.2%; L-leucine 800 μM achieved only 28.5% restoration (8× higher dose, lower efficacy).
- In rat dexamethasone-induced atrophy model (IV, 100 mg/kg/day, 14 days): gastrocnemius mass rescue 19.3%; grip strength 90% of normal; treadmill endurance 85% of normal — all superior to oral L-leucine.
- RNA-seq: 41.7% reversal of dexamethasone-induced transcriptomic changes; safety NOAEL = 2,000 mg/kg; therapeutic index >30. No human trials reported.

### [EXPRESS: Rapamycin Increases Cerebral Blood Flow and Modulates Metabolic, Inflammatory, and Microbiome Profiles in Healthy Middle-Aged APOE4 Carriers: A Pilot Single-Arm Trial](https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261490342)
⭐⭐⭐ · Aware C et al. · J Cereb Blood Flow Metab (2026) · n = 23 · single-arm pilot trial

- Single-arm, open-label pilot; low-dose rapamycin (sirolimus) 1 mg/day for 4 weeks; cognitively normal adults aged 45–65 years, stratified by APOE genotype (9 APOE4 carriers, 14 non-carriers).
- Primary outcome: cerebral blood flow (CBF); secondary outcomes: plasma metabolomics, inflammatory cytokines, Alzheimer's disease biomarkers, gut microbiome composition.
- APOE4 carriers: rapamycin significantly increased CBF, with increases exceeding 15% across multiple brain regions; minimal adverse effects reported.
- Non-carriers: no significant change in CBF; distinct physiological responses vs. carriers — genotype-dependent effect highlighted.
- Major limitations: no control arm, n = 9 in APOE4 subgroup, 4-week follow-up only; results are hypothesis-generating and cannot establish causality or long-term safety.

### [Electroacupuncture in Hypertension-Mediated Organ Damage: A Hypothesis-Generating Framework of Blood Pressure-Dependent and Candidate Blood Pressure-Independent Mechanisms](https://doi.org/10.1080/10641963.2026.2728373)
⭐⭐⭐ · Pang J et al. · Clin Exp Hypertens (2026) · narrative mechanistic review

- Narrative mechanistic review synthesizing preclinical and clinical literature from PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and CNKI on electroacupuncture (EA) and hypertension-mediated organ damage (HMOD).
- Preclinical evidence (predominantly spontaneously hypertensive rats): EA attenuates central sympathetic outflow, modulates brainstem-hypothalamic autonomic circuits, downregulates renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system signaling, reduces oxidative stress and inflammation.
- Organ-level evidence graded: moderate for cardiac remodeling (left ventricular hypertrophy, myocardial fibrosis); low-to-moderate for conduit-artery vascular changes; low for renal and cerebral effects.
- Critical methodological gap: no study has used BP-matched controls to isolate a BP-independent organ-protective component; no human trial with validated HMOD surrogate endpoints identified.
- Review conclusion: dual-pathway framework (BP-dependent + candidate BP-independent tissue pathways) is hypothesis-generating only; BP-independent organ protection remains unproven.

### [Iodine Supplementation for Preventing Iodine Deficiency Disorders in Children and Adolescents](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD014475.pub2)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Velasco I et al. · Cochrane Database Syst Rev (2026) · n = 2,528 (8 RCTs) · meta-analysis

- Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis; 8 RCTs (2,528 participants total); trials conducted across 7 countries between 1982 and 2009; follow-up 3–22 months.
- Dosing varied: 150 μg/day for 28 weeks (one study); single-dose 100 mg in infants <1 year; single-dose 400–490 mg in children aged 6–17 years.
- Cognitive function: SMD 0.19 (95% CI 0.04–0.34; 1 study, n=166; **high-certainty evidence**) — slight increase with iodine vs. placebo.
- Hypothyroidism: RR 0.03 (95% CI 0.00–0.23; 1 study, n=264; **moderate-certainty evidence**) — large reduction.
- All-cause mortality in infants: RR 0.32 (95% CI 0.14–0.73; 1 study, n=617; **moderate-certainty evidence**); goitre: very low-certainty evidence overall (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.15–3.07; I² = 97%); sensitivity analysis removing high-risk-of-bias study yields RR 0.31 (95% CI 0.22–0.45, moderate certainty).
- Growth (weight): little to no difference vs. placebo (MD −0.20 kg, 95% CI −1.92 to 1.52; low-certainty evidence); no adverse events related to iodine were reported across included trials.

### [Association Between Sleep Paralysis and Anxiety: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44470-026-00190-9)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Garrido G et al. · J Clin Sleep Med (2026) · n = 5,568 (11 studies) · meta-analysis

- PRISMA/Cochrane-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO: CRD42024579831); PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library searched; 11 eligible studies, 5,568 participants.
- Random-effects models (REML); pooled anxiety outcome: SMD 0.35 (95% CI 0.27–0.44; p < 0.01) — sleep paralysis associated with significantly higher anxiety scores vs. controls; heterogeneity I² = 0% (zero heterogeneity across studies).

- Sleep paralysis defined as REM-sleep parasomnia with temporary inability to perform voluntary movements during sleep-wake transition — mechanistic connection to anxiety may involve shared neural substrates of REM dysregulation.
- Causal direction not established by meta-analytic design; cross-sectional nature of included studies limits inference about whether anxiety predisposes to sleep paralysis or vice versa.

### [To Sleep and Dream: Unraveling Narcolepsy](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2622458123)
⭐⭐⭐ · Friedman JM · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2026) · perspective/review

- Perspective article in PNAS reviewing the convergent discoveries of Emmanuel Mignot and Masashi Yanagisawa establishing orexin (hypocretin) signaling as the pathogenic basis of narcolepsy.
- Yanagisawa's approach: purification of brain ligands for orphan GPCRs → identification of orexin peptide → reverse genetics showing orexin-knockout mice develop narcolepsy.
- Mignot's approach: forward genetics identifying genetic defects in orexin receptor 2 (OX2R) in hereditable canine narcolepsy.
- Synthesis: orexin signaling in the brain maintains wakefulness; orexin deficiency causes narcolepsy with cataplexy, hallucinations, and sleep attacks; findings have enabled both wake-promoting (orexin receptor agonists) and sleep-inducing (orexin receptor antagonists) pharmacotherapy.
- Not an original research study; no primary data, effect sizes, or patient population reported — historical/mechanistic synthesis.

- Scar scores: fractional laser therapy statistically superior to microneedling (SMD = −0.99, 95% CI −1.83 to −0.15; p = 0.02).

- Subgroup analyses (CO₂ vs. non-CO₂; traditional vs. non-traditional fractional lasers) showed no clear or consistent significant differences; substantial heterogeneity across multiple outcomes; small sample sizes and possible reporting bias flagged by authors.
