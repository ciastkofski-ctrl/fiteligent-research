Fiteligent Research Digest — Week of 2026-07-03

TL;DR — Top 5 Highlights This Week

  1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Creatine beats the combo — A 52-RCT network meta-analysis finds creatine alone (SMD = −0.64 for sprint performance) delivers the most consistent anaerobic performance gains; adding beta-alanine provides no meaningful additional benefit over creatine monotherapy.
  2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Vitamin D and insulin resistance in PCOS — A 14-study meta-analysis (n = 1,856 women) reports a significant inverse correlation between serum 25(OH)D and insulin resistance (pooled r = −0.32, 95% CI −0.40 to −0.23, p < 0.001), especially in overweight/obese and Asian subgroups.
  3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Heavy lifting protects peri-menopausal bone and muscle — A 9-month RCT (80–85% 1RM + drop landings, twice weekly) increased total body lean mass by 0.90 kg (95% CI 0.08–1.72), leg power by 97 W (95% CI 2–193), and lumbar spine aBMD by 0.020 g/cm² versus waitlisted controls.
  4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Multivitamin and COVID-19 — In a large double-blind RCT (n = 21,442), daily MVM did not significantly reduce COVID-19 incidence (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81–1.07), but per-protocol analysis showed reduced odds of symptomatic COVID-19 (OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.37–0.99) in adherent participants.
  5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ PCOS and cognition: small, non-significant effects — A 22-study meta-analysis (136,008 women with PCOS, 333,895 controls) finds pooled correlations for executive function (r = −0.12), attention (r = −0.13), and working memory (r = −0.14) all cross zero — no reliable cognitive impairment signal.

Obesity & Metabolic Health

Alterations in cognitive function among reproductive-age women with polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome: A systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Rogers et al. · J Neuroendocrinol (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis; databases searched: Ovid MEDLINE, Ovid EMBASE, CINAHL; search cutoff June 23, 2025; 2,887 articles screened, 22 studies included.
  • Total sample: 136,008 women with PCOS and 333,895 women without PCOS across included studies.
  • Pooled correlations for cognitive domains: executive function r = −0.12 (95% CI −0.26 to 0.02), attention r = −0.13 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.14), working memory r = −0.14 (95% CI −0.31 to 0.05) — all confidence intervals cross zero, indicating non-significant associations.
  • Substantial heterogeneity observed in several domains, largely driven by individual outlying studies.
  • Risk of bias generally low; funnel plots with Egger's regression provided no evidence of publication bias.

Comparative risk of epilepsy with SGLT2 inhibitors versus incretin-based therapies in type 2 diabetes

⭐⭐⭐ · Yen et al. · Epilepsia (2026) · propensity-score matched cohort study

  • Nationwide real-world study using Taiwan's National Health Insurance Research Database, January 2000 to December 2021.
  • Propensity score matching produced: 20,096 matched pairs of SGLT2i vs. DPP-4 inhibitor users; 8,671 matched pairs of SGLT2i vs. GLP-1 RA users; 1,611 matched pairs of GLP-1 RA vs. DPP-4 inhibitor users.
  • SGLT2i vs. DPP-4 inhibitors: adjusted HRs were 0.49 for seizure, 0.38 for epilepsy, and 0.46 for the composite seizure/epilepsy outcome (log-rank p < 0.001 for all cumulative incidences).
  • SGLT2i vs. GLP-1 RAs: lower risk of composite seizure/epilepsy outcome (aHR = 0.66, p = 0.016); no significant difference for individual seizure or epilepsy outcomes separately.
  • No significant differences found between GLP-1 RAs and DPP-4 inhibitors; observational design precludes causal inference.

Longevity

The dual role of mTOR in multiple sclerosis pathophysiology: a systematic review

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Koks et al. · J Neurol (2026) · systematic review

  • PRISMA-compliant systematic review; 189 records identified, 90 met inclusion criteria for qualitative analysis; included in vitro, in vivo (predominantly rodent models, both sexes), and MS patient tissue/data studies.
  • mTOR inhibition consistently reduced pro-inflammatory signaling and may enhance autophagy, offering neuroprotection across study models.
  • mTOR activation promotes remyelination by enhancing oligodendrocyte differentiation and maturation; however, in inflammatory environments, mTOR activation also supports immune cell expansion and glial reactivity, inducing inflammation and oxidative stress.
  • Authors conclude a dual, context-dependent role: stage of disease and timing of intervention are critical determinants of which mTOR effect predominates.
  • Authors flag that therapeutic mTOR modulation in MS is "unlikely to be readily clinically translatable without a substantial risk of unintended and context-dependent effects."

Indigenous perspectives on healthy aging in the Yukon

⭐⭐⭐ · Christopher et al. · Int J Circumpolar Health (2026) · qualitative study

  • Commissioned by the Canadian Frailty Network; grounded in Indigenous research methodologies and Two-Eyed Seeing; guided by an Elder co-author.
  • 42 participants aged ≥55 years recruited across four Yukon communities via guided conversations.
  • Activity, Interaction, and Diet were raised spontaneously by participants as central to healthy aging; Vaccinate and Optimize medications domains typically required prompting.
  • Participants consistently reframed these domains in relational and place-based terms: wellness located in connection to land, traditional foods, intergenerational responsibility, and community.
  • Authors identify that the AVOID Frailty model's emphasis on disease prevention and individual behaviour change does not account for collective, relational, and cultural dimensions shaping Indigenous health.

Multimetal-Doped Nanoenzymes Reprogram Macrophages for Immunotherapy of Gouty Arthritis

⭐⭐⭐ · Zhou et al. · Adv Healthc Mater (2026) · preclinical animal/in vitro study

  • Preclinical study; polydopamine-based nanozyme developed with high superoxide dismutase and catalase activities via bimetallic ion coordination; loaded with uricase, rapamycin, and hyaluronic acid.
  • In a gouty arthritis animal model, hyaluronic acid targeted CD44 receptors on macrophage surfaces, enhancing nanoprobe accumulation at the joint.
  • Nanoprobe significantly eliminated monosodium urate crystals via multienzyme cascade reactions; levels of IL-1β and IL-6 were significantly reduced; NF-κB signaling pathway was effectively suppressed.
  • Macrophage phenotype shifted from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2; rapamycin suppressed immunogenicity of uricase and enhanced in vivo therapeutic efficacy.
  • No human data; findings are preclinical only and require validation in clinical trials.

Strength & Hypertrophy

Preventing muscle and bone loss in peri-menopausal and early post-menopausal females with heavy strength and impact training: Results from the STOP-EM randomised waitlisted controlled feasibility trial

⭐⭐⭐ · Whitman et al. · Bone Rep (2026) · randomised waitlisted controlled feasibility trial

  • STOP-EM feasibility RCT; 9-month, twice-weekly, supervised progressive heavy strength training (80–85% 1RM) plus impact training (drop landings); conducted in Calgary, Canada.
  • Baseline characteristics: mean age 52.9 years (SD 4.1); 47.5% peri-menopausal; 7.5% on menopausal hormone therapy; 20 intervention participants and 18 waitlisted controls, all returned at follow-up.
  • 1RM strength improved by 12.6% to 30.5% across chest press, shoulder press, deadlift, and back squat lifts in the intervention group.
  • Between-group differences for change: total body lean mass +0.90 kg (95% CI 0.08–1.72); absolute leg power output +97 W (95% CI 2–193); lumbar spine aBMD +0.020 g/cm² (CI not fully reported in abstract).
  • Small feasibility sample; authors state these promising results warrant a definitive trial to assess program efficacy.

Clinical and donor-site outcomes of DIEP versus TRAM flaps in post-mastectomy breast reconstruction: a systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Urquijo Méndez et al. · J Plast Surg Hand Surg (2026) · systematic review and meta-analysis

  • PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review; databases: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science; studies published January 2000–December 2025; 10 studies met inclusion criteria, 5 contributed quantitative data to the meta-analysis.
  • Pooled analysis showed no significant difference in fat necrosis between DIEP and TRAM flaps (OR 0.86; 95% CI 0.54–1.36; I² = 34.6%); total flap loss rates were similarly comparable (OR 0.79; 95% CI 0.39–1.61; I² = 7.2%).
  • DIEP reconstruction was consistently associated with fewer donor-site morbidities and significantly better maintenance of abdominal wall health compared with TRAM flap.
  • Exploratory network meta-analysis: TUG flap showed superiority in reduced total flap loss; DIEP flap ranked favorably in donor-site outcomes and patient satisfaction.
  • No single technique consistently outperformed all others across all outcomes; authors recommend individualized surgical approach based on patient anatomy, comorbidities, reconstruction goals, and surgeon expertise.

Note: This study pertains to reconstructive surgery and is included for completeness; its primary clinical relevance is outside core fiteligent themes.

Supplements

Effects of combined versus single supplementation of creatine and beta-alanine on aerobic and anaerobic performance: a systematic review and network meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Bai & Xu · J Int Soc Sports Nutr (2026) · network meta-analysis

  • Network meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines; databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (to August 2025); 52 RCTs with PEDro score ≥6 included; minimum supplementation duration ≥2 weeks; participants: healthy adult athletes.
  • Outcomes assessed: sprint performance, jump, agility, upper-body muscular endurance (UME), lower-body muscular endurance (LME), and repeated-sprint ability (RSA); standardized mean differences (SMD) computed within random-effects models.
  • Creatine supplementation significantly improved sprint performance (SMD = −0.64); specific SMDs for other outcomes not fully extracted in available abstract text.
  • Co-supplementation of creatine + beta-alanine offers no additional advantage beyond creatine monotherapy for the outcomes assessed; beta-alanine's buffering advantage described as "task-specific and insufficient to augment Cr's ergogenic efficacy."
  • Authors call for standardized, long-term, multi-arm RCTs to clarify potential interactive mechanisms and sex- or sport-specific responses.

Association Between Serum Vitamin D Levels and Insulin Resistance in Women With Polycystic Ovary Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Guo et al. · J Obstet Gynaecol Res (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis; databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library; search cutoff December 2025; 14 observational studies included; total n = 1,856 women with PCOS.
  • Outcome: correlation between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] and insulin resistance indices (HOMA-IR/fasting insulin); pooled correlation coefficients calculated using random-effects models.
  • Significant inverse correlation found: pooled r = −0.32 (95% CI −0.40 to −0.23; p < 0.001; I² not specified in abstract excerpt).
  • Subgroup analyses: association was particularly pronounced in overweight/obese women and in Asian populations.
  • Observational design; association does not establish that vitamin D supplementation would reduce insulin resistance in PCOS — interventional RCTs required to confirm causality.

Multivitamin supplementation and COVID-19 incidence and symptom severity in the COcoa Supplement and Multivitamin Outcomes Study (COSMOS) randomized trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Li et al. · Am J Clin Nutr (2026) · RCT (secondary analysis)

  • Secondary analysis of COSMOS: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial; original trial period June 2015–December 2020; primary trial enrolled 21,442 US adults for cardiovascular disease and cancer prevention.
  • COVID-19 analysis window: January–December 2020; 18,205 participants remained adherent as of January 2020; 382 MVM and 404 placebo participants reported COVID-19 infection.
  • Intention-to-treat analysis: HR for COVID-19 incidence MVM vs. placebo = 0.93 (95% CI 0.81–1.07) — not statistically significant.
  • Among 338 cases with detailed symptom data: OR for symptomatic COVID-19 (ITT) = 0.70 (95% CI 0.44–1.11); in per-protocol analysis among compliant participants only, OR = 0.60 (95% CI 0.37–0.99).
  • Limitations: COVID-19 outcomes were not the primary endpoint of COSMOS; trial ran only through December 2020 (pre-vaccination, pre-Delta/Omicron); self-reported infection; results apply to generally healthy older US adults.

Sleep & Recovery

Insomnia and poor sleep quality in refugee and asylum-seeking populations: A systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Carpinelli et al. · PLoS One (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis; databases: Cochrane, Embase, PubMed; search from inception to December 2024; 66 studies included; total n = 42,956 participants.
  • Pooled mean Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) score: 13.76 (95% CI 10.39–17.13) — upper end of subthreshold range, bordering moderate clinical insomnia.
  • Pooled mean Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) score: 8.59 (95% CI 2.11–15.07) — exceeds the clinical threshold for poor sleep (>5).
  • Pooled prevalence of sleep adversities: 43.2% in adults and 36.4% in children; secondary findings include prolonged sleep latency and frequent nightmares.
  • Substantial sample heterogeneity limited statistical significance in subgroup analyses across populations and assessment methods.

Esketamine improves postoperative sleep quality in thoracic surgery patients with preoperative sleep disturbance: a randomized controlled trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Wang et al. · Front Pharmacol (2026) · RCT · n = 100

  • Single-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; 100 patients undergoing elective non-cardiac thoracic surgery under general anesthesia with pre-existing sleep disturbance; intervention: 0.25 mg/kg esketamine at anesthesia induction and at cutaneous suture vs. placebo.
  • Primary outcome — incidence of postoperative sleep disturbance on POD 1: esketamine 50% vs. control 82% (OR 0.2; 95% CI 0.1–0.5; p = 0.001); POD 3: 22% vs. 42% (OR 0.4; 95% CI 0.2–0.9; p = 0.032); difference no longer significant by POD 5.
  • Esketamine group showed significant differences in sleep duration, proportion of deep sleep, and proportion of REM sleep on POD 1 and POD 3 (Fitbit-derived estimates described as exploratory).
  • Inflammatory biomarkers: serum IL-6 lower in esketamine group on POD 1 and 3; IL-10 higher at all three postoperative time points; BDNF concentrations consistently higher in esketamine group across all postoperative assessments.
  • Intraoperative sufentanil consumption was significantly lower in the esketamine group; anxiety and depression scores (HADS) were significantly lower in esketamine group on POD 1, 3, and 5; single-center design limits generalizability.

A narrative review on the interplay between interstitial lung disease and obstructive sleep apnea: Mechanisms, clinical implications, and management strategies

⭐⭐⭐ · Ocal et al. · Sleep Med X (2026) · narrative review

  • Narrative review examining the epidemiology, mechanisms, clinical implications, and management of OSA in the context of interstitial lung diseases (ILDs).
  • OSA described as highly prevalent in ILD even among non-obese patients; driven by restrictive mechanics, diminished lung volumes, impaired diaphragmatic function, and severe nocturnal hypoxemia — creating a distinct OSA phenotype from classic presentations.
  • Proposed mechanisms linking OSA and ILD: intermittent hypoxia, recurrent arousals, and autonomic activation; available evidence is largely observational; independent causal contribution of OSA beyond ILD severity and nocturnal hypoxemia described as "uncertain."
  • Standard screening tools and home sleep apnea testing flagged as potentially inadequate for detection of the ILD-OSA phenotype; REM-related oxygen instability and sleep fragmentation noted as specific concerns.
  • PAP therapy (with supplemental oxygen when needed) may improve sleep quality, fatigue, daytime symptoms, and nocturnal oxygenation; whether this translates to slower ILD progression or improved survival described as "unproven."

Skin & Hair Health

Effects of low-level laser therapy on the aging process of human dermal fibroblast cells

⭐⭐⭐ · Dehkordi et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · in vitro study

  • In vitro study; human dermal fibroblasts (HDFs) exposed to UV-A (365 nm) and red light (650 nm) individually or sequentially; cell viability assessed via MTT assay and flow cytometry; gene expression assessed by qPCR.
  • UV irradiation temporarily reduced cell viability at 24 h; sequential treatment with red light after UV-A (UL group) resulted in partial recovery; S-phase cell percentage was higher in the UL group at 72 h (p < 0.05), indicating increased proliferative activity.
  • Expression of Col1A1 (collagen synthesis) and Dnmt3b (DNA methylation marker) increased (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01, respectively) in the UL group; apoptotic markers (Bax, Bcl2, Casp8) were altered (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01), suggesting reduced apoptosis.
  • No clinical data; all findings are in vitro only; authors note further studies are required to optimize treatment protocols and validate clinical applications.
  • Study does not report sample sizes (n) for cell culture replicates in the available abstract.

Neutrophilic differentiation of promyelocytic HL-60 cells is associated with increased expression of terminal galactose residues on the cell surface

⭐⭐⭐ · Timoshenko & Kay · Exp Cell Res (2026) · in vitro study

  • In vitro study using human leukemia HL-60 cells; ATRA-induced neutrophilic differentiation (1 μM ATRA, 3 days); lectin-induced aggregation assay used to analyze changes in terminal galactose residues on the cell surface.
  • Ricinus communis agglutinin (RCA)-induced aggregation activity was enhanced by a sialyltransferase inhibitor (P-3FAX-Neu5Ac) and suppressed by an N-glycosylation inhibitor (tunicamycin).
  • Following ATRA-induced differentiation, RCA-induced aggregation was elevated, implying increased terminal galactose residues on the cell surface.
  • Bioinformatics analysis of GEO datasets suggested this change may be associated with metabolic reprogramming of sialyltransferases and neuraminidases controlling availability of galactose-terminated glycans.
  • Basic cell biology study; no direct skin or hair clinical relevance; included due to automated theme classification.

ATPR promotes differentiation in gastric cancer cells by endocan

⭐⭐⭐ · Zhong et al. · Mol Biol Rep (2026) · in vitro study

  • In vitro study; gastric cancer cells (SGC-7901) treated with ATPR (4-amino-2-trifluoromethyl-phenyl retinate), a derivative of all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) with reportedly lower cytotoxicity; Hoechst staining, alkaline phosphatase (AKP) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity measured, along with endocan mRNA and protein levels.
  • ATPR treatment resulted in significant reduction in LDH and AKP activities and a marked increase in endocan expression at both mRNA and protein levels.
  • In stable endocan-knockdown gastric cancer cells, LDH and AKP activities were significantly restored, suggesting endocan is necessary for ATPR's pro-differentiation effect.
  • Mechanism proposed: ATPR promotes gastric cancer cell differentiation via upregulation of endocan; study does not include in vivo or clinical data.
  • Classified under skin & hair by automated theme guessing due to retinoid compound class; primary relevance is oncology, not dermatology or hair biology.
