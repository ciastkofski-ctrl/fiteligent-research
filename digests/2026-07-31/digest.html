Obesity & Metabolic Health

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy and GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Timing: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Pisani D et al. · Endocrine (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis of 3 retrospective cohort studies (10,880 pregnancies: 4,942 GLP-1 RA–exposed, 5,938 unexposed) identified from 75 records across Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, and ClinicalTrials.gov (inception through December 15, 2025).
  • All 3 included studies were conducted in the United States (2014–2025) and covered semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, tirzepatide, exenatide, lixisenatide, and albiglutide exposures during periconception or first trimester.
  • Pooled OR for HDP in exposed vs. unexposed: 0.91 (95% CI 0.57–1.47); result not statistically significant; heterogeneity direction differed across studies (2 showing lower risk, 1 showing higher risk).
  • Random-effects Mantel-Haenszel model used; risk of bias assessed with ROBINS-I tool; all included studies were observational, precluding causal inference.
  • Authors conclude that available evidence is insufficient to establish or exclude an association; large prospective studies are required.

The Emerging Role of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists in Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension: Therapeutic Potential and Challenges

⭐⭐⭐ · Thandampallayam M et al. · Indian J Ophthalmol (2026) · review

  • Narrative review evaluating GLP-1 RA mechanisms and evidence in idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH), a condition for which clinical guidelines recommend ≥10% total body weight loss as a disease-modifying target.
  • GLP-1 RAs proposed to reduce intracranial pressure (ICP) via two mechanisms: (1) weight loss–mediated ICP reduction, and (2) direct effects on cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) production independent of weight.
  • Ongoing studies cited suggest GLP-1 RAs may reduce reliance on acetazolamide (standard CSF-reducing pharmacotherapy); no quantitative trial data are reported in this review.
  • Key challenges noted: absence of IIH-specific RCT data, high drug cost, side-effect profile (nausea, GI), and necessity of long-term use given chronic nature of IIH.
  • Authors position GLP-1 RAs as adjunctive to—not replacements for—multidisciplinary lifestyle management; larger dedicated trials identified as the evidence gap.

Early Changes in Renal Function Do Not Appear to Mediate Cardiovascular Risk with Dapagliflozin Plus Semaglutide: A Real-World Observational Hypothesis-Generating Cohort Study

⭐⭐⭐ · AlTaweel MO et al. · Expert Rev Cardiovasc Ther (2026) · retrospective cohort

  • Retrospective cohort of 376 adults prescribed dapagliflozin ± semaglutide at a Saudi tertiary hospital (2020–2024); follow-up window 90–365 days; inverse probability of treatment weighting and causal mediation analysis applied.
  • Primary question: whether early ΔeGFR mediates the association between dapagliflozin + semaglutide combination therapy and HF/MI-related clinical outcomes.
  • Combination therapy vs. dapagliflozin alone: adjusted RR 0.787 (95% bootstrap CI 0.320–1.813); not statistically significant.
  • Early ΔeGFR showed little evidence of mediating HF/MI-related risk differences between the two treatment arms.
  • Authors flag major limitations: outcomes not independently adjudicated, single-center Saudi population, hypothesis-generating only; larger prospective trials needed.

Longevity

Brazilin Attenuates Methylglyoxal-Induced Endothelial Injury by Enhancing eIF5A Hypusination and Inhibiting the AMPK/mTOR-Mediated Autophagy/Apoptosis Axis in Vitro and in Vivo

⭐⭐⭐ · Liang Y et al. · Int J Mol Med (2026) · preclinical (in vitro + in vivo)

  • Mechanistic study investigating brazilin (BZ), a natural compound, as a cytoprotective agent against methylglyoxal (MGO)-induced endothelial injury; MGO is a major advanced glycation end-product (AGE) precursor implicated in diabetic vascular complications.
  • BZ proposed mechanism: allosteric activation of deoxyhypusine hydroxylase (DOHH) → enhanced eIF5A hypusination → suppression of AMPK/mTOR signaling → reduced MGO-triggered autophagy and apoptosis in endothelial cells.
  • In vitro, the effect of BZ on MGO-induced autophagy/apoptosis was potentiated by 3-methyladenine and Compound C (autophagy/AMPK inhibitors), and abolished by ciclopirox (a specific DOHH inhibitor), confirming target specificity.
  • In vivo validation used db/db mice (diabetic microenvironment model); DOHH inhibition reversed BZ-mediated suppression of autophagy and apoptosis in this model.
  • No human or clinical data; entirely preclinical; translational relevance to be established.

Cryoelectron Tomography Reveals an Age-Related Decline in Mitoribosomes That Contributes to T Cell Dysfunction in Older Individuals

⭐⭐⭐ · Chen J et al. · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2026)

  • Used cryoelectron tomography to resolve the structure and spatial organization of mitochondrial ribosomes (mitoribosomes) in primary human CD8⁺ T cells across different age groups.
  • Study addresses a species-specific evidence gap: mouse models have established mitochondrial dysfunction as a driver of T cell aging, but whether equivalent mitochondrial alterations occur in human T cells remained unclear and contested.
  • Key finding: age-related decline in mitoribosome abundance documented in primary human CD8⁺ T cells (full quantitative data not available in the provided abstract excerpt).
  • Methodology (cryoelectron tomography) allows near-native structural resolution of organellar complexes within intact cells, representing a technical advance over prior indirect measures of mitochondrial function.
  • Translational implications for immunosenescence and aging-associated immune decline; no intervention tested.

Amelioration of Cancer Cachexia-Induced Muscle Atrophy and Adipose Tissue Wasting by a Combination of Amaranth Hydrolysate and Korean Mint Extract in BALB/c Mice

⭐⭐⭐ · Lee J et al. · Food Sci Nutr (2026) · preclinical (animal model)

  • BALB/c mouse model of cancer cachexia (CC) used to evaluate a combination of Amaranthus caudatus hydrolysate and Agastache rugosa (Korean mint) extract on muscle atrophy and adipose tissue wasting.
  • Cancer cachexia characterized in the study as a multifactorial metabolic syndrome involving systemic inflammation, muscle wasting, and adipose depletion; a significant unmet clinical need.
  • Specific quantitative effect-size data on muscle mass or adipose preservation not reported in the provided abstract excerpt.
  • Animal-only study (BALB/c mice); combination of plant-derived compounds limits individual-component attribution; dose and route of administration not specified in the abstract.
  • No human data; findings are hypothesis-generating for future nutritional or nutraceutical interventions in cachexia.

Strength & Hypertrophy

Unstable Trunk Core Strength Training Improves Shooting Performance and Holding Stability in Elite 10 m Air Pistol Athletes: A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Jin R et al. · Sci Rep (2026) · RCT

  • Pilot RCT; 20 national-level air pistol shooters randomized to 8 weeks of unstable trunk core strength training (UTCST; n = 10; Swiss balls, BOSU balls, balance discs) or stable surface training (STCST; n = 10).
  • Primary outcomes: center-of-pressure (COP) parameters via plantar pressure analysis and shooting performance parameters via SCATT laser system, recorded 1 second before trigger pull.
  • UTCST vs. STCST: COP sway area −11.03% vs. −2.02% (p < 0.05, η²p = 0.22); COP path/area ratio +9.55% vs. +3.61% (p < 0.05, η²p not fully reported in excerpt).
  • Shooting performance improvement direction favored UTCST; full effect-size data for performance endpoints truncated in provided abstract.
  • Limitations: pilot design (n = 20), single sport (precision shooting), sport-specific equipment (unstable surfaces); generalizability to conventional strength or hypertrophy populations is limited.

Comparative Efficacy of an 8-Week Core Stability Program versus Foot-Ankle Strengthening on Pain, Function, and Distal Structural Parameters in Individuals with Knee Osteoarthritis: A Randomized Controlled Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Nazir SNB & Asim A · Sci Rep (2026) · RCT

  • RCT; 100 participants aged ≥40 years with Kellgren-Lawrence grade II–III knee OA randomized to core stability exercise (CSE, n = 50) or foot-ankle strengthening (FAS, n = 50), both added to conventional knee exercises 3×/week for 8 weeks.
  • Primary outcome: resting pain intensity (VAS); secondary outcomes included KOOS subscales, 40-m fast-paced walk, 30-second chair stand, 11-step stair climb, Achilles tendon thickness, and navicular/foot ratio; ANCOVA adjusted for baseline used.
  • Between-group differences exceeded MCID for resting pain and all KOOS subscales in favor of CSE; MCID thresholds met for walk and chair-stand tests; MDC surpassed for stair-climb and distal structural parameters; large effect sizes reported for core stability training.
  • Authors caution that distal structural modifications (Achilles tendon thickness, navicular/foot ratio) are exploratory and likely reflect localized soft-tissue fluid shifts rather than permanent anatomical remodeling; advanced imaging validation needed.
  • Two-group design; 8-week follow-up only; no active follow-up beyond intervention end; single-center; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06766877.

Delaying Task Failure in High-Intensity Exercise: A Pi-Afferent-Effort Framework for Targeted Sports Nutrition

⭐⭐⭐ · Stout JR et al. · J Int Soc Sports Nutr (2026) · narrative review

  • Hypothesis-generating narrative review proposing the Pi-afferent-effort model of task failure, in which inorganic phosphate (Pi) accumulation from rapid ATP turnover and phosphocreatine breakdown converges with neural, perceptual, and behavioral limits to terminate high-intensity exercise.
  • Model identifies Pi as impairing crossbridge function and Ca²⁺ handling, with downstream effects on group III/IV muscle afferent signaling and perception of effort.
  • Framework applied to performance nutrition targeting: creatine (phosphocreatine resynthesis), beta-alanine (pH buffering via carnosine), caffeine (central afferent modulation), and sodium bicarbonate (systemic acidosis buffering) discussed as mechanistically aligned candidates.
  • No original experimental data; no effect sizes, n, or p-values reported; purely theoretical synthesis.
  • Authors call for future studies combining metabolic, neuromuscular, perceptual, and performance outcomes to distinguish whether supplements reduce work-matched strain, increase tolerance of terminal strain, or both.

Supplements

The Impact of Omega Fatty Acids on DKD: A Multimodal Study Integrating Mendelian Randomization, Proteomic Mediation Analysis, and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Zhang L et al. · FASEB J (2026) · meta-analysis + Mendelian randomization

  • Two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) using GWAS data and plasma proteomic panels; two-step protein-mediated MR framework applied; Reactome pathway enrichment used to map biological mechanisms; followed by meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (474 participants) on omega-3 supplementation in DKD.
  • MR result: genetically predicted higher plasma omega-3 levels causally associated with reduced DKD risk (OR 0.869, 95% CI 0.772–0.978, p = 0.020); omega-6 showed no significant association (OR 0.895, 95% CI 0.731–1.096, p = 0.283).
  • Fourteen plasma proteins showed nominally significant partial mediation; mediation proportions ranged 2.10%–29.80%; candidate mediators included neuronal pentraxin-2, hemoglobin subunit theta-1, and periostin; enriched pathways: Notch signaling, apoptosis regulation, chronic inflammation.
  • Meta-analysis (12 RCTs, 474 participants): omega-3 supplementation reduced triglycerides (MD = −0.27 mmol/L, 95% CI −0.35 to −0.20, p < 0.00001), systolic BP (MD = −4.50 mmHg, 95% CI −7.57 to −1.42, p = 0.004), and kidney injury molecule-1 (MD = −1.74 pg/mL, 95% CI −2.58 to −0.89, p < 0.0001); HDL-C increased (MD = +0.14 mmol/L, 95% CI 0.04–0.23, p = 0.004).
  • No significant improvement in albumin-to-creatinine ratio or eGFR in the meta-analysis; small pooled RCT sample (474) limits power; observational MR cannot fully exclude confounding by linkage disequilibrium.

Probiotic Supplementation in Children and Adolescents with ADHD: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of ADHD-Related and Emotional-Behavioral Outcomes

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Yan Y et al. · Nutrients (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (482 participants under 18 years with DSM-defined ADHD); intervention durations 8–12 weeks; databases searched through October 2025 (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, PsycINFO, EBSCO); random-effects models used.
  • Primary result: probiotic supplementation did not show a clear benefit on ADHD-related clinical outcomes in the broad exploratory synthesis (SMD = −0.25, 95% CI −0.57 to 0.07); result not statistically significant.
  • Exploratory finding: probiotic supplementation may improve broad parent-reported emotional-behavioral outcomes, particularly Conners' Parent Rating Scale (CPRS)-assessed behavioral symptoms; benefit not consistently supported by Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) outcomes.
  • Authors explicitly flag that any emotional-behavioral signal should be interpreted with caution given small number of heterogeneous trials and lack of consistency across outcome tools.
  • Larger, rigorously designed RCTs identified as necessary; no serious adverse events reported in included trials.

Efficacy and Safety of Prebiotics, Probiotics, and Synbiotics on Hemoglobin and Anemia in the Pediatric Population: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Elías Solís GE et al. · PLoS One (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (4,188 children); PubMed, Embase, and CENTRAL searched to August 2025; GRADE used to rate certainty of evidence; protocol pre-registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251108276).
  • Prebiotics, probiotics, and synbiotics (alone or combined with iron) showed no clinically important effects on hemoglobin, hematocrit, or ferritin vs. placebo or standard care; GRADE certainty rated high to very low depending on comparison.
  • One RCT (probiotics vs. placebo) found a potential reduction in anemia incidence (RD −6 per 100, 95% CI −15 to +6, low certainty); confidence interval crosses zero, making this finding inconclusive.
  • Safety profile: no serious adverse events reported; observed events were mainly mild gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Authors recommend larger high-quality trials in high-risk pediatric populations; current evidence does not support routine use of pre/pro/synbiotics to improve iron status in children.

Sleep & Recovery

Effects of Physical Exercise on Sleep Quality in Patients with Psychiatric Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Wang M et al. · BMC Psychiatry (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (24 comparisons, 1,628 participants) with psychiatric disorder diagnoses; databases searched to March 1, 2026 (MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science); risk of bias assessed with Cochrane RoB 2; Hedges' g used as effect measure; random-effects models.
  • Overall effect: physical exercise significantly improved sleep quality vs. control (Hedges' g = −0.57, 95% CI −0.75 to −0.38, p < 0.001); classified as a moderate effect.
  • FITT subgroup analyses: largest effects observed for ≥3 sessions/week (g = −0.60), aerobic exercise modality (g = −0.69), and low-to-moderate intensity (g = −0.70); single sessions ≥30 min (g = −0.58) and intervention duration <12 weeks (g = −0.58) also associated with larger effects.
  • Univariate meta-regression found no significant linear moderating effects of intervention duration, frequency, volume, or total weekly exercise time on sleep quality (all p > 0.05), suggesting that these categorical subgroup differences should be interpreted cautiously.
  • Substantial heterogeneity expected given varied psychiatric diagnoses, exercise protocols, and sleep outcome measures across included trials; diagnostic grouping not disaggregated in available abstract data.

Efficacy of an Anonymous, Preventive, Personalized Online Counselling to Improve Shift Workers' Sleep Quality: A Randomized Controlled Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Retzer L et al. · Sci Rep (2026) · RCT

  • Prospective RCT in German shift workers across various industries (intention-to-treat sample n = 66); intervention group received 4 personalized CBT-based online counselling messages over 4 weeks + sleep diaries; waitlist control completed sleep diaries only; registered in DRKS (DRKS00017777).
  • Primary outcome: sleep efficiency (self-reported via sleep diary); secondary outcomes: insomnia symptoms, depression symptoms, daytime sleepiness.
  • Both intervention and control groups showed significant improvements in sleep efficiency and insomnia/depression symptoms; daytime sleepiness did not improve in either group.
  • Key finding: improvements in the counselling group were not significantly greater than in the waitlist control group; superiority of the CBT-based counselling intervention was not demonstrated.
  • Authors propose that repeated sleep diary completion and study participation may themselves have had therapeutic effects; limitations include small sample (n = 66), 4-week active intervention, and lack of objective sleep measurement.

Interaction of Person-Affect-Cognition-Execution-Based Digital Interventions for Sleep Procrastination in Chinese University Students: Pilot Randomized Study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Xu H et al. · JMIR Form Res (2026) · pilot RCT

  • Pilot RCT; 46 Chinese university students with high sleep procrastination allocated to online (n = 16), offline (n = 16), or control (n = 14) groups; 8-week intervention grounded in the I-PACE (Interaction of Person-Affect-Cognition-Execution) model; subjective and objective (wearable) outcomes measured at baseline, post-intervention, and 2-month follow-up.
  • Subjective sleep procrastination: all three groups improved similarly; no significant group × time interaction detected.
  • Objective sleep procrastination (wearable-measured sleep onset): significant group × time interaction (Wald χ²₁₆ = 28.79, p = 0.03); online group showed numerically earlier sleep onset in later intervention weeks; online group mean approached zero at week 7 (−0.05 h) but did not differ significantly from zero (p = 0.96) and rebounded at week 8.
  • No between-group comparisons survived Bonferroni correction at any single time point; all pairwise findings are exploratory.
  • Pilot design limits power; small group sizes (n = 14–16); population restricted to Chinese university students; I-PACE mediation pathways not formally tested.

Skin & Hair Health

Short-Term Efficacy and Safety of 5 MHz Fractional Microneedle Radiofrequency for Facial Rejuvenation: A Prospective Study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Qiu H et al. · Ann Med (2026) · prospective study

  • Single-arm prospective study; 20 participants with facial aging received a single full-face 5 MHz fractional microneedle radiofrequency (FMR) treatment; assessments at baseline and days 1, 4, 7, 14, and 28 using MC750 and VISIA imaging systems.
  • Primary outcomes: skin physiological parameters (hydration, melanin, hemoglobin, elasticity), VISIA absolute parameters, and physician global assessment (PGA); secondary outcomes: adverse events and patient satisfaction.
  • Skin hydration: transient early decline followed by gradual recovery; melanin: stable throughout; hemoglobin: brief early increase before returning to baseline; skin elasticity: improved significantly at visit 2 (day 7) and remained elevated at visits 5–6.
  • VISIA: wrinkle scores showed sustained improvement during follow-up; greater baseline wrinkle severity associated with greater wrinkle improvement; no significant differences across Fitzpatrick skin types or age subgroups.
  • No control arm; n = 20; 28-day maximum follow-up; only transient erythema and burning reported; no serious adverse events, including no post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation; 85% of participants reported being satisfied or very satisfied.

All-Trans Retinoic Acid plus Eltrombopag for Refractory/Relapsed Immune Thrombocytopenia

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Fu H et al. · NEJM Evid (2026) · RCT

  • Multicenter, randomized, open-label trial; 96 adults with glucocorticoid-resistant or relapsed immune thrombocytopenia (ITP; platelets <30×10⁹/L) randomized 1:1 to ATRA + eltrombopag (n = 48) or eltrombopag monotherapy (n = 48); 12-week ATRA course; 18-month follow-up.
  • Primary endpoint (18-month sustained response): 60% (29/48) in combination group vs. 35% (17/48) in monotherapy group (OR 2.78, 95% CI 1.22–6.37, p = 0.014).
  • Complete response rate higher in combination arm (79% vs. 58%; 95% CI for difference 2 to 39 percentage points); median response duration longer (75 vs. 37 weeks; HR for relapse 0.45, 95% CI 0.23–0.86).
  • Adverse events comparable between groups; no grade 3–4 events and no treatment-related deaths reported; note: 21% of combination group had clinically significant bleeding (WHO grade 2–3) at baseline vs. 10% in monotherapy group, indicating baseline imbalance.
  • Open-label design introduces performance bias risk; primarily relevant to hematology/immunology (included under skin/hair given retinoid pharmacology and platelet-related dermatological implications).

Treatment of Striae Albae with Combination of Fractional Radiofrequency and Topical Tretinoin: A Randomized Controlled Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Grove GL et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · RCT

  • RCT with within-patient 4-area design; 20 patients each had 4 anatomically comparable striae areas randomized to: (i) topical tretinoin (TT), (ii) fractional radiofrequency (FRF), (iii) FRF + TT combination, (iv) untreated control; 3 treatment sessions 1 month apart; TT applied between sessions; 20-week follow-up; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05461755.
  • Primary outcome: change in POSAS (Patient Observer Scar Assessment Scale) SUM score (range 6–60) from baseline to 20-week follow-up; assessed by both patient (POSAS-PT) and blinded dermatologist (POSAS-OBS).
  • Baseline POSAS-PT SUM medians: 24–25 (moderate-severe); at follow-up, patient-reported improvement (ΔSUM 5–10 points across areas); only combination FRF + TT was significantly better than untreated control (p = 0.029).
  • Blinded dermatologist assessments (POSAS-OBS): ΔSUM 0–2 points across all treatments; no treatment reached significance vs. control (all p ≥ 0.269); 3D photographs showed only subtle tendencies.
  • Within-patient design controls for inter-individual variability but limits sample independence; n = 20; 20-week endpoint; striae albae remain treatment-resistant overall; findings support combination as the most promising single approach tested.
