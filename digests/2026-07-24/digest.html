  1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ICS therapy loses superiority in obese/overweight asthmatic children — A systematic review of 9 studies (1 RCT, 5 cohorts, 3 case-control) finds that inhaled corticosteroid monotherapy or step-up regimens, the standard pharmacological backbone of pediatric asthma management, appear to lose their comparative advantage over other controllers in children with overweight or obesity.   2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Vitamin D (4,800 IU/day × 8 weeks) reduced anxiety but not overall depression scores in youth aged 10–24 — In a partially randomized trial (n = 108), randomized vitamin D supplementation did not significantly improve Hamilton Depression Rating Scale scores vs placebo (B = −0.30, p = 0.698), but did reduce anxiety on the GAD-7 (B = −1.78, 95% CI −3.35 to −0.32, p = 0.026). A subgroup with moderate-to-severe depression showed benefit.

  3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Poor sleep quality affects 67% of tension-type headache patients — A meta-analysis of 27 observational studies finds a pooled prevalence of poor sleep quality (PSQI) of 67% (95% CI 53–79%) in adults with tension-type headache, with strong associations with depressive symptoms and headache burden.

  4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Plant-forward diet in toddlers accelerates bone turnover without altering nutrient intake — A 10-month cluster-RCT in Finnish ECEC centres (n = 116 children) finds elevated bone formation (S-BAP, p = 0.04) and resorption markers (S-TRAP5b, p = 0.02) in the plant-forward group vs controls, with no detectable change in calcium, vitamin D, phosphorus, or protein intake. Clinical significance for long-term bone health remains unclear.

  5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Dutasteride vs finasteride: comparable hair density gains, stronger PSA suppression with dutasteride — A 24-week single-blind RCT in 46 Iranian men with moderate-to-severe AGA finds no significant difference in trichoscopic hair density or thickness between finasteride 1 mg/day and dutasteride 0.5 mg/day, but dutasteride produced a greater serum PSA reduction (from 0.63 ± 0.18 to 0.42 ± 0.22).

Obesity & Metabolic Health

Comparative Response to Pharmacological Management of Asthma in Overweight/Obese Children versus Their Counterparts: A Systematic Review and Call for Data

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Castro-Rodriguez et al. · Eur Respir Rev (2026) · Systematic review (narrative synthesis; 9 studies: 1 RCT, 5 cohorts, 3 case-control)

  • Systematic search of four bibliographic databases from inception to January 2025 identified studies examining asthma treatment effects stratified by weight status (overweight/obese vs. normal/underweight) in children.
  • Nine studies were included; meta-analysis was not conducted due to methodological heterogeneity across study designs, outcome definitions, and weight classification criteria.
  • Narrative synthesis indicates that the superiority of inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) monotherapy or step-up therapy over other controllers appears to be lost in overweight/obese children compared to their normal-weight counterparts.
  • Authors explicitly state that "no firm recommendations could be made regarding potential differential treatment responses," characterizing findings as hypothesis-generating.
  • The review constitutes a call for targeted RCT data on this subpopulation; the single eligible RCT had methodological limitations preventing pooled inference.

Genetic Architecture Shared Between Body Shape Phenotypes and Preeclampsia-Related Diseases: A Genome-Wide Cross-Trait Analysis

⭐⭐⭐ · Shan et al. · J Obstet Gynaecol (2026) · Genome-wide cross-trait / GWAS analysis

  • Used GWAS summary statistics and multiple analytical approaches (LDSC, HDL, genetic covariance analysis, pleiotropy-informed methods) to assess shared genetic architecture between adiposity-related traits and preeclampsia (PE)-related diseases.
  • Bidirectional Generalised Summary-data-based Mendelian Randomisation (GSMR) was applied to evaluate causal relationships between adiposity phenotypes and PE.
  • Identified 35 independent significant causal SNPs, 32 enriched tissues, and 7 high-confidence shared genes supported by Bayesian colocalization (COLOC) analysis.
  • Positive genetic correlations and robust causal associations were found between adiposity-related traits and PE-related diseases; shared gene functional enrichment characterized via Gene Ontology and KEGG pathways.
  • Study is GWAS-based; no sample size for an original cohort is applicable; findings are mechanistic/genetic and do not constitute clinical guidance.

Comparison of Lifestyle, Surgery, and Semaglutide for Weight Management in Endometrial Cancer: A Prospective Observational Study

⭐⭐⭐ · Wei et al. · J Obstet Gynaecol (2026) · Prospective observational cohort

  • Three-arm prospective study in patients with endometrial cancer receiving fertility-sparing treatment: sleeve gastrectomy (n = 53), semaglutide therapy, and the DEAR (Diet, Exercise, Accompany and Refresh) lifestyle programme; exact group sizes for semaglutide and DEAR not fully extractable from abstract.
  • At 1 year, all three groups achieved weight loss; sleeve gastrectomy produced the greatest BMI reduction compared with semaglutide (difference = 4.04 kg/m²).
  • DEAR programme demonstrated better medium-term weight maintenance and metabolic stability compared to semaglutide at the medium-term follow-up assessment.
  • Tumour response favoured the DEAR programme over semaglutide in the medium term; sleeve gastrectomy showed rapid short-term superiority for weight reduction.
  • Observational design without randomization; confounding by indication is a major limitation; sample sizes per arm are not clearly delineated in the abstract.

Longevity

Gestational Protein Restriction Promotes Osteoporotic Phenotype During Aging

⭐⭐⭐ · Calsa et al. · Calcif Tissue Int (2026) · Animal study (mouse model)

  • C57BL/6J mice were subjected to gestational low-protein (LP) dietary restriction; male offspring were evaluated at 12 and 18 months of age using micro-CT, three-point bending mechanical testing, ultrastructural analysis, RNA sequencing, RT-qPCR, and immunohistochemistry.
  • At 18 months, LP offspring showed significant reductions in trabecular bone volume fraction, bone mineral density, and trabecular number; reduced cortical thickness was observed at both 12 and 18 months.
  • Three-point bending tests demonstrated reduced maximal load in LP-18M femora, indicating compromised mechanical strength.
  • Ultrastructural analyses revealed increased cortical porosity and disrupted collagen fiber organization in aged LP bone; RNA-seq identified enriched pathways for macrophage recruitment, complement signaling, and osteoclast differentiation.
  • RT-qPCR confirmed increased expression of C5ar1, Ccl3, Ccr2, Lilrb4a, and Trem2; immunohistochemical analysis showed increased CD68+ macrophage infiltration; findings are limited to a male mouse model and require human validation.

mTOR Signaling Contributes to System-Driven Rhythmic Gene Expression in Mouse Liver

⭐⭐⭐ · Sahasrabudhe et al. · Sci Adv (2026) · Basic science / animal study (mouse)

  • Study investigated the role of mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) signaling in food-driven hepatic rhythmic gene expression independently of intrinsic circadian clocks, using mouse liver models.
  • mTOR signaling was demonstrated to be both necessary and sufficient to mediate food-driven rhythmic gene expression, rhythmic regulation of the liver metabolome, and the endoplasmic reticulum stress response in hepatic tissue.
  • Acute mTOR inhibition administered before the active phase desynchronized the phase of mTOR-driven rhythmic genes without affecting clock-controlled rhythmic genes, indicating phase-specific sensitivity.
  • Findings suggest that misalignment between circadian clock signals and systemic mTOR-driven signals may mechanistically contribute to metabolic disease phenotypes associated with circadian disruption (e.g., shift work, jet lag).
  • Authors propose that mTOR inhibitors could potentially be used to resynchronize system-driven rhythms; all findings are in murine models; human translational relevance is speculative at this stage.

Distinct Roles of COPI Proteins Attenuated in Cell Senescence

⭐⭐⭐ · Mazan-Mamczarz et al. · Sci Adv (2026) · Basic science / cell biology (WI-38 human fibroblasts)

  • RNA interference screen in WI-38 human fibroblasts was used to identify how silencing individual subunits of the coatomer protein I (COPI) complex — critical for Golgi-to-ER intracellular transport — affects cellular function in proliferating vs. senescent cells.
  • In proliferating fibroblasts, silencing COPA, COPB1, COPB2, or COPD induced ATF4 production and disrupted autophagy, apoptosis, and cytokine signaling, consistent with impaired Golgi-to-ER retrograde transport.
  • Silencing COPG1, COPE, or COPZ1 in proliferating cells altered the production of extracellular matrix proteins, indicating subunit-specific functional specialization within the COPI complex.
  • In senescent WI-38 fibroblasts, silencing individual COPI proteins did not elicit the same phenotypes observed in proliferating cells, indicating that COPI-dependent homeostatic transport is attenuated during cellular senescence.
  • Study is conducted entirely in a single human fibroblast cell line; in vivo and tissue-level relevance, and implications for organismal aging, remain to be established.

A 10-Month Cluster-Randomized Trial to Shift Towards Plant-Forward Meals in Early Childhood Education and Care Centres: Effects on Bone and Mineral Metabolism in Finnish Children

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Itkonen et al. · Eur J Nutr (2026) · Cluster-RCT · n = 116 (individual-level, blood subsample n = 46) · RCT

  • 10-month cluster-RCT: 11 ECEC centres randomized to plant-forward meals (more plant-based products, less red meat, moderation in milk encouraged; n = 55 children at individual level) vs. 12 control centres maintaining regular meals (n = 61 children); complete blood and dietary data from 18 intervention and 28 control children.
  • Plant-forward group had higher bone formation marker S-BAP vs. control (diff = 0.05 log-U/L, 95% CI 0.004–0.104; p = 0.04), higher bone resorption marker S-TRAP5b (diff = 2.12 U/L, 95% CI 0.35–3.90; p = 0.02), and higher total S-ALP (diff = 0.034 log-U/L, 95% CI 0.002–0.066; p = 0.04).
  • No significant differences between groups in serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D, plasma parathyroid hormone, or any nutrient intakes (calcium, vitamin D, phosphorus, protein).
  • Mean S-25(OH)D concentrations were adequate (≥ 50 nmol/L) in both groups.
  • Authors note accelerated bone turnover without a detected dietary mechanism; clinical relevance for long-term bone health in 3–5-year-olds requires further investigation; blood subsample (n = 46) is small and limits statistical power. Registered: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05249946.

Distinct Effects of Supplementation with Resistant Starch and Polydextrose on Plasma and Faecal Bile Acid Profile and Associations with Gut Microbiota: A Randomised, Controlled Intervention in Healthy Participants

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Fan et al. · Eur J Nutr (2026) · Double-blind RCT (2×2 factorial) · n = 74 (plasma), n = 50 (faeces)

  • Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled 2×2 factorial trial; healthy participants consumed 23 g/day Hi-maize resistant starch (RS) and/or polydextrose (PD) or placebo; bile acid (BA) profiles measured by ultra-performance liquid chromatography mass spectrometry in plasma (n = 74) and faeces (n = 50).
  • RS supplementation (not PD) increased a range of conjugated BAs and deoxycholic acid in plasma (FDR < 0.05); taurochenodeoxycholic acid concentrations positively correlated with Akkermansia abundance in response to RS (FDR = 0.027); taurine-conjugated BAs also correlated positively with Akkermansia (FDR = 0.049).
  • Neither RS nor PD altered total BA concentrations in faeces; RS decreased faecal proportions of primary BAs (p = 0.032) and PD increased them (p = 0.012).
  • PD reduced secondary BA transformation ratios (p < 0.05) along with shifts in related microbial associations, indicating a distinct mechanism of action from RS.
  • Negative correlations between plasma primary conjugated BAs and faecal secondary BAs were observed in response to RS specifically (p < 0.05); healthy-participant population limits direct extrapolation to clinical conditions. Registered: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01214681.

The Impact of Vitamin D Supplementation on Depression and Anxiety in Young Individuals with Depression and Vitamin D Insufficiency/Deficiency

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Lee et al. · Eur J Nutr (2026) · Partially randomized patient preference trial · n = 108

  • Partially randomized patient preference trial; outpatients aged 10–24 with major depressive disorder and vitamin D insufficiency/deficiency; participants expressing preference received vitamin D (preference arm, PVD, n = 28); others randomized to vitamin D (RVD, n = 41) or placebo (PL, n = 39); supplementation: 4,800 IU vitamin D3/day for 8 weeks.
  • Primary outcome (assessor-rated, 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale): no significant difference between RVD and PL (B = −0.30, 95% CI −1.81 to 1.21, p = 0.698).
  • Secondary outcome, anxiety (GAD-7): reduced in the RVD group vs. placebo (B = −1.78, 95% CI −3.35 to −0.32, p = 0.026).
  • Subgroup analysis: participants with moderate-to-severe depression in the RVD group showed reduced depression and anxiety vs. placebo; however, the adolescent subgroup (10–19 years) with moderate-to-severe depression did not differ significantly from placebo on any outcome score.
  • No significant differences in hypercalcemia or hyperphosphatemia between groups; sample size constraints preclude definitive conclusions; authors recommend future confirmatory trials.

Advances in Chrysin Research for Cardiovascular Disorders: Mechanistic Insights and Therapeutic Prospects

⭐⭐⭐ · Singh & Solanki · Mol Biol Rep (2026) · Narrative review

  • Narrative review summarizing preclinical research on chrysin (5,7-dihydroxyflavone), a flavonoid found in fruits, plants, and vegetables, in the context of cardiovascular disorders including myocardial infarction, atherosclerosis, hypertension, cardiotoxicity, and diabetic cardiovascular complications.
  • Proposed cardioprotective mechanisms include: (1) reduction of oxidative stress via upregulation of endogenous antioxidant enzymes (SOD, catalase, glutathione); (2) anti-inflammatory effects via suppression of IL, NF-κB, TNF-α, and COX-2; (3) improvement of endothelial function through lipid metabolism regulation and increased endothelial nitric oxide; (4) reduction of apoptosis via downregulation of pro-apoptotic markers.
  • In models of myocardial ischemia and cardiac hypertrophy, chrysin was associated with reduced infarct size, reduced vascular proliferation, and enhanced cardiac function in preclinical studies.
  • Key signaling pathways modulated include PI3K/Akt/Nrf2, MAPK, NOX4, and TGF-β/Smad.
  • Review is entirely preclinical/mechanistic; no human RCT data are presented; clinical applicability cannot be inferred from this evidence base.

Sleep & Recovery

Sleep Disturbances in Tension-Type Headache: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Yuan et al. · J Neurol (2026) · Systematic review and meta-analysis · 27 observational studies

  • Systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library from inception to January 3, 2026; included 27 observational studies reporting sleep outcomes in adults with tension-type headache (TTH).
  • Primary outcome: pooled prevalence of poor sleep quality (PSQI) in TTH = 67% (95% CI 53–79%; significant between-study heterogeneity noted, I² not fully reported in abstract excerpt).
  • Poor sleep quality was particularly prevalent among TTH patients presenting in clinical (vs. community) settings.
  • Poor sleep quality was associated with depressive symptoms; exploratory associations with headache burden (frequency, severity) were also reported.
  • Authors recommend sleep assessment as part of routine clinical evaluation for TTH; all included studies are observational, precluding causal inference regarding direction of the sleep–headache relationship.

The Incidence of Sleepiness in Newer-Generation Antihistamine Users: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Leelakanok et al. · Eur J Clin Pharmacol (2026) · Systematic review and network meta-analysis · 163 studies

  • Systematic search of CINAHL Complete, Medline, Scopus, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar from inception to July 2025; 163 studies included; pairwise meta-analysis used binary random-effects model; network meta-analysis in frequentist framework.
  • Overall pooled incidence of sleepiness in newer-generation antihistamine users: 1.3% (95% CI 1.0–1.6%).
  • All nonsedating antihistamines administered at standard doses showed a higher prevalence of sleepiness compared to placebo; however, most individual prevalence values were not statistically significantly different from placebo.
  • Study is relevant to populations self-administering antihistamines for allergy and expecting full wakefulness (e.g., drivers, shift workers, patients operating machinery).
  • Heterogeneity (I²) was evaluated; exact subgroup-level estimates by antihistamine agent are not reported in the available abstract excerpt.

Effects of Andropause-Related Health Education on General Health and Sleep Quality Among Retired Men: A Quasi-Experimental Study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Maghsoudi et al. · Sleep Breath (2026) · Quasi-experimental (pretest-posttest with control) · n = 60

  • Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest study conducted October 2022–March 2024 at Neyshabur University of Medical Sciences, Iran; 60 retired male employees (30 per group) selected by stratified quota + simple random sampling.
  • Intervention: four-week virtual training program delivered via WhatsApp covering andropause-related health topics; control group received standard treatment only; outcomes measured at baseline, post-intervention, and 2-month follow-up using GHQ (General Health Questionnaire) and PSQI.
  • General health: significant improvement in intervention group (p < 0.001); control group unchanged (p = 0.834). Repeated-measures ANOVA: significant time effect (p < 0.001, η² = 0.232) and time × group interaction (p < 0.001, η² = 0.226).
  • Sleep quality: significantly improved in intervention group (p = 0.002); control unchanged (p = 0.621). ANOVA: significant time effect (p = 0.011, η² = 0.087) and group effect (p = 0.006, η² = 0.123).
  • Limitations: quasi-experimental design (not double-blind RCT); single institution; exclusively Iranian male population; WhatsApp delivery may not generalize across healthcare systems; no objective sleep measures used.

Skin & Hair Health

Efficacy and Safety of Oral Finasteride versus Dutasteride in Moderate-to-Severe Androgenetic Alopecia in Males Based on Trichoscopic and Laboratory Findings: A Clinical Comparison in Iran

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Poostiyan et al. · J Dermatolog Treat (2026) · Randomized single-blind clinical trial · n = 46

  • Single-blind RCT; 46 Iranian men aged 20–50 years with moderate-to-severe androgenetic alopecia (AGA); randomized to finasteride 1 mg/day or dutasteride 0.5 mg/day for 24 weeks.
  • Primary endpoints: hair density and thickness measured by trichoscopy and standardized photographs; satisfaction assessed by visual analog scale (VAS); adverse effects documented.
  • Both groups showed improvement in hair growth at 24 weeks; no statistically significant difference was observed between groups in trichoscopic hair density or thickness.
  • Serum PSA fell in the dutasteride group from 0.63 ± 0.18 to 0.42 ± 0.22, a greater reduction than in the finasteride group, consistent with more potent dual 5-alpha-reductase isoenzyme inhibition by dutasteride.
  • Limitations: single-blind design (not double-blind); small sample (n = 46); single-center Iranian population; 24-week duration may be insufficient to detect between-group differences in clinically meaningful hair density outcomes.

Effect of Infrared Technology on Dermal Thickness of the Face Compared to Microfocused Ultrasound Using High Frequency Ultrasound Skin Imaging: A Randomized Clinical Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Stefanello et al. · J Cosmet Laser Ther (2026) · RCT · n = 30

  • Randomized trial in 30 healthy women; 1:1 randomization to Zaffiro® infrared (IR) technology or microfocused ultrasound (MFU); primary outcome was dermal thickness measured by high-frequency ultrasound skin imaging; secondary outcomes included skin firmness and elasticity metrics.
  • Both interventions targeted facial skin rejuvenation via non-ablative thermolifting mechanisms; device-specific parameters, number of sessions, and follow-up duration are not fully described in the available abstract.
  • Study evaluated comparative effectiveness of two energy-based devices for facial skin aging; detailed effect size data and between-group statistical comparisons are not reported in the available abstract excerpt.
  • Small sample (n = 30, 15 per arm) substantially limits statistical power and generalizability.
  • Randomized design is methodological strength; absence of a sham/untreated control arm limits interpretation of absolute device efficacy.

Facial Contour Modulation and Skin Tightening Using 40.68-MHz Unipolar Radiofrequency

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Kim et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · Prospective single-arm interventional clinical study · n = 30 enrolled, n = 26 completed

  • Prospective single-arm study; adults with mild-to-moderate facial laxity underwent three full-face 40.68-MHz unipolar radiofrequency (RF) sessions at 4-week intervals; 26 of 30 enrolled participants completed follow-up to Week 20.
  • Primary endpoint (VECTRA 3D analysis at Week 20): modest volume reductions in cheek (−2.2 mL), jowl (−1.7 mL), and submental region (−2.9 mL) from baseline.
  • Skin elasticity parameters (Cutometer): R2 increased from 0.62 to 0.69; R5 increased from 0.53 to 0.59 at Week 20.
  • Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS): improvement observed in 88.5% of participants; mean FACE-Q score = 72.4 ± 10.1; mean VAS pain score = 1.2 ± 0.9; only transient erythema and mild edema reported; no serious adverse events.
  • Uncontrolled single-arm design without a comparator or placebo group; authors explicitly state findings are preliminary and require confirmation in controlled trials with longer follow-up.
