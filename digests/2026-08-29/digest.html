Obesity & Metabolic Health

Relationship Between Sleep Duration and Obesity in Children Aged 6–12 Years and Adolescents Aged 13–18 Years: A Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Hayli et al. · An Acad Bras Cienc (2026) · meta-analysis

  • Systematic search across PubMed, ProQuest, Google Scholar, EBSCO, and Web of Science for articles published 2010–2024; 1,116 articles identified, 17 systematically reviewed, 10 included in the meta-analysis.
  • Primary finding: combined OR for short sleep duration and obesity = 1.25 (95% CI: 1.12–1.42) across the full 6–18 age range.
  • Subgroup (children 6–12 years): combined OR = 1.20 (95% CI: 1.08–1.42).
  • Subgroup (adolescents 13–18 years): combined OR = 1.38 (95% CI: 1.18–1.63) — the stronger association in the older age group.
  • Limitation: total pooled n not reported in the abstract; heterogeneity statistics not provided; observational designs within the included studies preclude causal inference.

Vitamin D Supplementation and Central Adiposity in Male Shift Workers: A Randomized Clinical Trial on Metabolic Health

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Menezes-Júnior et al. · Sao Paulo Med J (2026) · n = 198 · RCT

  • Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; 198 male shift workers employed at an iron ore extraction company in Minas Gerais, Brazil; inclusion required 25(OH)D < 30 ng/mL plus ≥1 metabolic abnormality (metabolic syndrome, impaired fasting glucose, dyslipidemia, elevated BP, or elevated WC).
  • Three arms: placebo, cholecalciferol 14,000 IU/week, or cholecalciferol 28,000 IU/week; duration = 4 months.
  • Primary outcomes (WC and WHtR): no significant differences between either supplementation group and placebo (p > 0.05 for both outcomes at 4 months).
  • 25(OH)D increased significantly: +6.23 ng/mL (IQR: 0.58–10.61) in the 14,000 IU/week group; +15.25 ng/mL (IQR: 10.57–19.68) in the 28,000 IU/week group; placebo showed −0.50 ng/mL (IQR: −1.03 to 2.71).
  • Registered in ReBEC (RBR-6CS352, October 2017); all-male, single occupational setting limits generalizability to other populations.

Adipose-Cartilage Communication via EV-Mito-mtDNA Signaling Promotes Osteoarthritis Progression

⭐⭐⭐ · Gu et al. · Sci Adv (2026-08-28) · study type not reported

  • Mechanistic study: adipocyte-derived extracellular vesicles enriched in mitochondrial components (EV-Mito) transfer mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) to chondrocytes in both murine and human models.
  • Internalized mtDNA activates the cGAS-STING cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway, triggering inflammatory signaling, metabolic dysfunction, senescence, and cartilage degeneration.
  • Genetic and pharmacological inhibition of cGAS-STING signaling attenuated cartilage damage, pain behaviors, and gait abnormalities in mouse models.
  • Population-level and interventional data reported: body fat percentage (rather than BMI) showed a closer association with OA risk and severity.
  • In an exercise cohort, HIIT selectively reduced adiposity and was associated with lower mtDNA abundance in synovial EV fractions enriched for adipose-associated EV markers, reduced synovial 2′,3′-cyclic GMP-AMP levels, and superior improvement in OA symptoms compared with other interventions.

Lesion-Level Subtypes of White Matter Hyperintensity Evolution Beyond Spatial Location

⭐⭐⭐ · Gonzalez-Gomez et al. · Neurology (2026-09-22) · n = 403 · cohort

  • Longitudinal observational study: 3,224 MRI scans from 403 participants spanning cognitively normal aging, mild cognitive impairment, Alzheimer's disease, and Parkinson's disease; baseline + 2-year follow-up using structural, diffusion, and resting-state MRI.
  • 2,107 WMH lesions identified; unsupervised clustering of lesion-wise longitudinal changes yielded three distinct lesion subtypes with differential neurobiological and clinical significance.
  • Results indicate WMHs are not a homogeneous entity; lesion subtype composition was associated with neurodegeneration and vascular risk factors in multivariable models with FDR correction.
  • Three subtypes (labels not fully specified in the abstract) differ in progression trajectories, suggesting lesion composition provides more informative risk stratification than global WMH burden alone.
  • Associations assessed with multivariable models and FDR correction; specific effect sizes not provided in the abstract.

Platelet-Derived Serotonin Drives Age-Related Lung Pathology in SARS-CoV-2 Infection

⭐⭐⭐ · Marotta et al. · Sci Immunol (2026-08-28) · study type not reported

  • Mouse model of age-associated SARS-CoV-2 severity: platelet serotonin release upon activation was enhanced in middle-aged mice and aged human healthy donors.
  • Activated platelets aggregated and promoted fibrin deposition in lung microvasculature, associated with increased morbidity and respiratory dysfunction.
  • Pharmacologic or genetic disruption of serotonin uptake, or blockade of serotonin-dependent signaling, attenuated platelet activation and protected against respiratory distress independently of viral replication and immune responses.
  • Inhibition of fibrin formation similarly reduced disease severity, implicating serotonin-driven platelet procoagulant activity as a key contributor to age-related lung dysfunction.
  • Findings are predominantly preclinical (murine); translation to human therapeutic applications requires further clinical validation.

Strength & Hypertrophy

The Effect of Integrating Pain Neuroscience Education with Stabilization Exercises Using a Flexi-Bar on Functional Disability, Pain, and Geometry of Abdominal and Multifidus Muscles in Patients with Chronic Non-Specific Low Back Pain

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Dehghani et al. · PLoS One (2026) · n = 48 · RCT

  • 48 participants with chronic non-specific low back pain randomized to (1) pain neuroscience education (PNE) + Flexi-Bar stabilization exercises or (2) Flexi-Bar stabilization exercises alone; 3 sessions/week for 6 weeks.
  • Primary outcome (Roland-Morris Questionnaire for functional disability) and secondary outcome (pain intensity): significant Time × Group interaction effects, main effects of time, and main effects of Group (p < 0.05) favoring the combined PNE + exercise group.
  • Ultrasound showed significant post-intervention increases in Transverse Abdominis and lumbar Multifidus muscle thickness across supine, seated, and standing positions (p < 0.05); PNE + exercise group showed significantly greater increases vs exercise-only.
  • Secondary outcomes also included kinesiophobia, pain catastrophizing, and fear-avoidance beliefs, measured at baseline, post-intervention, and 4-week follow-up.
  • Limitation: no PNE-only group, precluding isolation of the educational component's unique contribution; trial registered prospectively (IRCT20230505058095N1, Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials).

Electromyographic Respiratory Muscle Analysis in the Spontaneous Breathing Trial: A Randomized Crossover Trial Comparing T-Piece and Pressure Support

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Bordin et al. · Crit Care Sci (2026) · n = 16 · RCT

  • Randomized crossover trial; 16 patients weaning from mechanical ventilation; each underwent two 30-minute spontaneous breathing trials (T-piece vs. pressure support ventilation at 7 cmH₂O) separated by ≥30-minute washout.
  • Surface EMG assessed pectoralis major, rectus abdominis, and external intercostal muscles at T0, T15, and T30 (% root mean square activation and median frequency).
  • Median frequency remained stable for all muscles in both conditions (p > 0.05), indicating no electromyographic fatigue over 30 minutes.
  • During T-piece trial: progressive increase in % RMS in pectoralis major, rectus abdominis, and external intercostals (p < 0.05); under pressure support, only pectoralis major showed a modest increase (p < 0.05), other muscles stable.
  • Intergroup delta comparisons (ANCOVA, adjusted for baseline): greater activation during T-piece for pectoralis major (p = 0.048) and rectus abdominis (p = 0.009); no significant difference for external intercostals.

Note: This study concerns mechanical ventilation weaning in ICU patients. It is categorized under Strength & Hypertrophy based on its EMG muscle analysis methodology but is primarily a critical care investigation; applicability to fitness or sports science is indirect.

  • Interventions reviewed: omega-3 fatty acids, ketogenic dietary interventions, B-vitamin supplementation, vitamin D, and microbiota-targeted strategies.

Palmitoylethanolamide Supplementation Partly Modulates Eccentric Exercise-Induced Changes in the Bioactive Lipid Profile of Skeletal Muscle Tissue

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Schouten et al. · Pflugers Arch (2026-08-28) · n = 10 · RCT

  • Double-blind crossover RCT; 10 healthy males received PEA (2 × 350 mg/d, Levagen+) or placebo (maltodextrin) in randomized order; muscle-damaging exercise = 24 × 10 eccentric contractions of the knee extensors on an isokinetic dynamometer.
  • Muscle biopsies collected at baseline and 48 h post-exercise; lipid mediator profile analyzed via LC-MS/MS-based lipidomics.
  • In the placebo condition, many lipid mediators decreased 48 h post-exercise; in the PEA condition, the same mediators remained unchanged or increased — pattern observed across mediators derived from AA (12-HHTrE, 11-HETE, 12-HETE, 15-HETE, 15-epi-LXA4, 14,15-EpETrE, 5,6-DiHETrE), DHA (4-HDoHE), LA (9-HODE, 13-HODE), and ALA (9-HOTrE, 13-HOTrE).
  • MCTR3 was consistently higher in PEA vs placebo; LXB4 and AT-RvD3 were consistently lower in PEA vs placebo, independent of exercise.
  • Very small sample (n = 10); no functional outcomes (soreness, strength recovery) reported in this analysis; results are mechanistic/lipidomic in nature.

PML-Driven Sumoylation of PML::RARA-Bound Repressors Drives Hematopoietic Progenitor Immortalization

⭐⭐⭐ · Wu et al. · J Exp Med (2026-09-07) · study type not reported
