Obesity & Metabolic Health

An Oxycodone-Based Conscious Analgesia Strategy with Supplemental Low-Dose Propofol Improves Safety in Elderly Obese Patients Undergoing Gastroscopy: A Prospective Randomized Controlled Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Chang H et al. · Drug Des Devel Ther (2026) · RCT

  • Prospective, single-center, open-label RCT enrolling 128 patients aged ≥65 years with BMI 30–39.9 kg/m²; all 128 completed the study.
  • Intervention: oxycodone-based conscious analgesia + supplemental low-dose propofol (group OP) vs. propofol-based deep sedation (group P).
  • Primary safety outcome: hypoxemia incidence — 10.94% (OP) vs. 37.50% (P); relative risk 0.29 (95% CI 0.14–0.63).
  • Group OP also showed fewer required airway interventions, less hypotension, lower total propofol exposure, and faster post-procedure recovery vs. group P.
  • Authors note these findings compare two sedation strategies only and do not establish improved respiratory function per se; single-center design limits generalizability.

Tirzepatide for treatment of postbariatric hypoglycemia after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass: a report of 3 cases

⭐⭐⭐ · Milosavljevic J et al. · JCEM Case Rep (2026) · Case Series (n = 3)

  • Three patients with symptomatic postbariatric hypoglycemia (PBH) following Roux-en-Y gastric bypass; presenting features included diaphoresis, visual disturbances, confusion, and documented postprandial low blood glucose.
  • Prior management included dietary modifications, acarbose, and other pharmacotherapies — all either partially effective or limited by side effects.
  • Tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist) initiation led to increased time in range, reduced number and severity of hypoglycemic episodes, and decreased glucose variability in all three cases.
  • Mechanism proposed: glucose-dependent insulinotropic activity of dual GLP-1/GIP agonism may attenuate postprandial hyperinsulinism driving PBH.
  • Case-series design (n = 3); no control condition; no quantitative effect sizes reported; authors call for prospective studies on efficacy and optimal dosing.

Relative fat mass predicts symptomatic knee osteoarthritis in older adults with metabolic syndrome: a longitudinal cohort study

⭐⭐⭐ · Huang L et al. · J Rehabil Med (2026) · Prospective Cohort

  • Prospective cohort using CHARLS data; 3,301 middle-aged and older Chinese adults with metabolic syndrome and no baseline knee osteoarthritis (KOA).
  • Primary outcome: incident symptomatic KOA; participants grouped into relative fat mass (RFM) quartiles.
  • KOA incidence ranged from 21% (Q1) to 37% (Q4); fully adjusted HR for highest vs. lowest RFM quartile = 1.50 (95% CI: 1.18–1.90), with a significant dose-response trend.
  • Restricted cubic spline analysis indicated a non-linear RFM–KOA association; the relationship was stronger among rural residents.
  • RFM outperformed BMI in predictive accuracy: AUC 0.587 vs. 0.532; Chinese-only sample limits generalizability to other populations.

Effect of yoga-based lifestyle intervention on endothelial function and vascular ageing in adults with type 2 diabetes: a study protocol for a randomised controlled (Yoga V-AgE) trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Patil SG et al. · BMJ Open (2026) · RCT Protocol (n = 248 planned)

  • Four-arm, parallel-group, prospective, open-label, blinded-endpoint RCT; 248 adults (62 per arm) aged 30–70 years with confirmed T2DM for ≥1 year; conducted at a tertiary care hospital in India.
  • Three yoga-based intervention arms receive different components of a yoga-based lifestyle program (supervised twice-weekly sessions + guided home practice over 12 weeks), plus standard medical care; control arm receives standard care only.
  • Primary outcome: change in endothelial function assessed by flow-mediated dilatation; secondary outcomes include arterial stiffness, vascular aging markers, glycaemic/lipid profiles, autonomic function, oxidative stress, quality of life, and cost of care.
  • Outcome assessments at baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months; intention-to-treat analysis; ANCOVA and mixed-effects models planned.
  • Published as a protocol only — no outcome data available; IEC-approved (SDMIEC/2025/1075); registered CTRI/2024/10/075712.

Critical role for the TGF-β1/mTORC1 signaling axis in defining the transcriptional identity of CTHRC1(+) pathologic fibroblasts

Fluorescent protein ticker tape (FPTT): Multiplexed recording of transcriptional dynamics in living cells and in vivo

⭐⭐⭐ · Wang R et al. · Sci Adv (2026) · Basic science / Tool development

  • FPTT is a fluorescent protein-based recording platform integrating multispectral monomeric fluorescent proteins with self-assembling protein fibers to log single-cell physiological activities longitudinally.
  • Demonstrated longitudinal recording of endogenous cFos transcriptional histories in hippocampal neurons at 3-hour resolution over 8 days in a dose-dependent and reversible manner.
  • Variants engineered for NF-κB, STAT3, mTOR, NFAT, and cAMP signaling pathways; applied to quantify cFos/NF-κB cross-talk in neurons and track STAT3/cAMP dynamics during mouse liver injury.
  • Simultaneous analysis of four major signaling pathways achieved during T cell activation; cell cycle-dependent oscillating mTOR dynamics characterized.
  • Entirely preclinical tool-development study; no human or clinical data; relevance to longevity biology is indirect via mTOR and inflammatory pathway monitoring.

Strength & Hypertrophy

Reliability, Device Agreement and Validity of Load-Velocity Profiles: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Claassen N et al. · Sports Med Open (2026) · Systematic Review & Meta-analysis

  • Preregistered (PROSPERO CRD42025634595) systematic review and meta-analysis; literature search in PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, and Scopus; 63 studies for sensor validity/reliability and 38 studies for 1RM prediction models.
  • Part I — Sensor validity and device agreement: pooled ICC = 0.91–0.92 (95% CI 0.83–0.97; k = 55 and 439, respectively); intra- and inter-day reliability ICC = 0.90–0.91 (95% CI 0.85–0.95; k = 228 and 608, respectively); linear position transducers (LPTs) showed more consistent performance than inertial measurement units (IMUs).
  • Part II — Velocity-based 1RM prediction: reliability ICC = 0.90 (95% CI 0.83–0.94; k = 124); validity ICC = 0.91 (95% CI 0.72–0.98; k = 9); large heterogeneity in lower-body exercise outcomes significantly biased results.
  • Moderators identified: exercise complexity, intensity, sensor technology, and modeling approach all influenced results; methodological quality assessed with adapted COSMIN.
  • Absence of measurement error and agreement analyses in primary studies limits definitive conclusions; authors recommend cautious, sensor- and exercise-specific interpretation of velocity-based monitoring and 1RM prediction.

Goal-directed nutritional support in preserving muscle mass and optimising recovery in treatment of oesophageal cancer: results of a prospective non-randomised cluster trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Barth I et al. · Support Care Cancer (2026) · Prospective Non-Randomised Cluster Trial (n = 100)

  • Non-randomised cluster trial; 50 patients per group — goal-directed nutritional support (GDNS) at a tertiary centre vs. usual care at a secondary hospital; all underwent neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (CRT) and oesophagectomy for oesophageal cancer.
  • Appendicular skeletal muscle index (ASMI) assessed by bioelectrical impedance analysis at baseline, CRT completion, pre-operatively, and 12 months postoperatively.
  • ASMI declined during CRT in both groups (GDNS: −2.53 ± 3.43%; usual care: −3.20 ± 6.92%; P = 0.559), partially recovered pre-operatively (GDNS: +0.95 ± 4.33% vs. usual care: +2.51 ± 7.95%; P = 0.368), then declined 12 months post-surgery (GDNS: −5.75 ± 4.75% vs. usual care: −7.09 ± 7.38%; P = 0.598).
  • No statistically significant between-group differences in ASMI at any timepoint after adjustment for confounders; one-year overall and disease-free survival did not differ between groups.
  • Non-randomised cluster design with center-level confounding (tertiary vs. secondary hospital); results may reflect institutional differences beyond nutritional protocol.
  • Authors conclude GDNS-associated ASMI losses were numerically smaller, but findings underscore need for personalised, integrated interventions — especially postoperative.

Six weeks of isometric resistance training led to evidence of corticospinal but not reticulospinal adaptation in previously untrained adult males

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Walker S et al. · Exp Brain Res (2026) · RCT (n = 39)

  • Randomised controlled trial; 39 healthy untrained males (~23 years); groups: sustained contraction (n = 13), explosive contraction (n = 9), control (n = 17); 6-week isometric resistance training intervention.
  • Both training groups showed significant improvements in maximal isometric torque (MVC: +27%, P < 0.01) and rate of torque development (RTD: +60%, P < 0.01); control group did not improve.
  • Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) revealed reduced motor-evoked potential (MEP) area (−21%, P < 0.01) and shortened silent period duration (−23%, P < 0.01) in training groups — interpreted as reduced cortical inhibition and corticospinal excitability.
  • The sustained contraction group showed reduced modulation of reaction time and increased MEP suppression due to loud sound (120 dB) in the StartReact test — indicating possible corticospinal rather than reticulospinal adaptation.
  • Conflicting changes in reticulospinal measures preclude clear conclusions; small explosive contraction group (n = 9), untrained males only, short training duration; authors recommend longer interventions and varied training modalities in future research.

Supplements

Effect of Post-Discharge Oral Nutritional Supplements on Nutritional Status in Patients after Gastrectomy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Xu W & Niu C · J Vis Exp (2026) · Systematic Review & Meta-analysis (7 RCTs; n = 1,736)

  • Systematic review of 7 RCTs (n = 1,736) identified from 5 databases through September 2025; all assessed oral nutritional supplementation (ONS) after hospital discharge in patients undergoing gastrectomy for gastric cancer.
  • ONS significantly attenuated absolute body-weight loss vs. control: WMD 0.75 kg (95% CI: 0.11 to 1.40).
  • ONS also significantly reduced percentage body-weight loss: WMD 1.42 (95% CI: 0.78 to 2.07).
  • No significant between-group differences detected for body composition, handgrip strength, or laboratory markers.
  • Adverse-event incidence was not significantly different between groups (RR: 1.17; 95% CI: 0.92 to 1.49); certainty limited by small number of trials, substantial heterogeneity in primary weight outcome, and absence of formal GRADE assessment; most outcomes assessed at 8 weeks–3 months only, so durability beyond this window is unknown.

Vitamin D Status and Supplementation in Lumbar Spinal Fusion: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Radiographic and Clinical Outcomes

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Kapetanakis S et al. · J Musculoskelet Neuronal Interact (2026) · Systematic Review & Meta-analysis (9 studies)

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis; databases searched from inception to January 2026 per PRISMA; 9 studies included; random-effects models used as primary analysis.
  • Five studies (n = 351) assessed radiographic fusion success: patients with better vitamin D status or supplementation had significantly higher likelihood of successful fusion (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.13–1.38; I² = 0%).
  • Six studies (n = 3,081) evaluated fusion failure: vitamin D-exposed groups had significantly lower failure risk (RR 0.45, 95% CI 0.27–0.76; I² = 34.8%).
  • Patient-reported outcomes (pain, function): no significant effects in random-effects analyses; changes below minimal clinically important differences.
  • Heterogeneity partly attributed to differences in outcome definitions and study design; authors call for prospective studies; sensitivity analyses identified these heterogeneity sources.

Effects of ketone supplementation on metabolic and hormonal profiles in polycystic ovary syndrome: A systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Khan SA et al. · Medicine (Baltimore) (2026) · Systematic Review & Meta-analysis

  • Systematic review and meta-analysis; inverse-variance (IV) random-effects models; heterogeneity assessed with I² and χ² tests; publication bias evaluated via funnel plots.
  • BMI: significant reduction following ketone supplementation (IV −13.08; 95% CI −17.47 to −8.69; P < 0.00001; I² = 0%); symmetrical funnel plots indicating low publication bias.
  • Fasting glucose: significant reduction (IV −10.83; 95% CI −12.98 to −8.68; P < 0.00001; I² = 93%); insulin: significant reduction (IV −11.87; 95% CI −15.30 to −8.44; P < 0.00001; I² = 93%).
  • Cholesterol: decreased, but not statistically significant (IV −10.77; 95% CI −26.84 to 5.30; P = 0.19; I² = 0%); LH: significant reduction (IV −5.30; P < 0.00001); androgen levels: significant reduction (IV −6.93; P = 0.0001); FSH and estrogen: significant increases (both P < 0.00001).
  • Very high heterogeneity (I² = 93%) in glucose and insulin outcomes substantially limits confidence in pooled estimates; total number of included studies and sample size not reported in the available record; "ketone supplementation" likely encompasses ketogenic diets and/or exogenous ketone bodies — specific intervention types not disambiguated in available abstract.

Sleep & Recovery

ENLIGHTENme: Translating the Benefits of Self-Administered Daytime Light Supplementation to Older Adults in the Real World

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Constantino DB et al. · J Pineal Res (2026) · RCT (n = 202)

  • Interventional, prospective, randomised study; 202 participants (63–92 years; 139 females) recruited across three EU cities at different latitudes: Amsterdam, Bologna, and Tartu.
  • Design: 2-week baseline assessment (wearables + daily diaries for light exposure, rest-activity patterns, sleep) → 12-week randomisation to indoor light supplementation (>8,000 melanopic EDI lamp in most-used room; n = 98) or no-intervention control → 2-week reassessment.
  • Greater naturalistic light exposure at baseline was associated with increased daytime activity, consolidated wakefulness, better subjective sleep quality, and reduced metabolic disorder incidence.
  • Individuals with lower baseline naturalistic light initiated supplementation earlier and for longer; supplementation onset before 10:00 was associated with greater daytime activity and lower rest-activity fragmentation.
  • Reported as a real-world feasibility and efficacy study; quantitative effect sizes for sleep and circadian outcomes not fully reported in the available abstract; multi-site design (3 latitudes) supports ecological diversity of findings.

The Effect of Yoga Breathing Exercise on Stress and Sleep Quality in Women With Breast Cancer Undergoing Chemotherapy

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Solmaz G et al. · Oncol Nurs Forum (2026) · RCT (n = 60)

  • Randomised, sham-controlled trial; 60 women with stage II–III breast cancer undergoing chemotherapy at a university hospital in Türkiye (November 2023 – April 2024); 1:1 allocation to yoga breathing exercise (YBE) or sham breathing group.
  • YBE protocol: 4-4-4-4 box breathing pattern, twice daily for 5 minutes, over 21 consecutive days; both groups received standard oncology care.
  • After 21 days, YBE group showed significantly greater reduction in perceived stress vs. sham (mean difference = −6.23, P < 0.001; measured by Perceived Stress Scale).
  • Sleep quality improvement was also significantly greater in YBE group vs. sham (mean difference = −2.1 on Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, P < 0.001; lower score = better quality).
  • No adverse events reported in either group; outcomes assessed using two-way repeated-measures ANOVA; short follow-up (21 days); single-center; exclusively female oncology population — generalizability to broader populations is limited.

Cross-cultural adaptation and construct validity of the Portuguese version of the Bordeaux Sleepiness Scale (BOSS): A cross-sectional study in patients with sleep disorders

⭐⭐⭐ · de Castro MM et al. · Pulmonology (2026) · Cross-sectional (n = 234)

  • Cross-sectional observational study; 234 adults attending a Sleep Disorders Clinic; 94.4% had obstructive sleep apnoea; 9.4% reported sleepiness-related accidents or near-misses in the previous year.
  • Portuguese BOSS translated via standardised cross-cultural adaptation process per COSMIN recommendations; participants completed Portuguese BOSS, Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS), and two driving-related sleepiness questions.
  • Internal consistency: Cronbach's α = 0.683 (moderate).
  • Criterion validity (ROC analysis): BOSS AUC = 0.895 vs. ESS AUC = 0.842 for identifying patients with sleepiness-related driving events; both scales showed significantly higher scores in participants with prior accidents/near-misses vs. those without.
  • Predominantly OSA sample limits generalizability to other sleep disorders; cross-sectional design; no test-retest reliability data reported.

Skin & Hair Health

Natural Agents for Preventing Skin Damage Induced by Visible Light: A Systematic Review of Preclinical and Clinical Evidence

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Rodríguez-Luna A et al. · Photodermatol Photoimmunol Photomed (2026) · Systematic Review

  • Comprehensive systematic review evaluating photoprotective effects of natural compounds against visible light (VL) and high-energy visible light (HEVL)-induced skin damage; preclinical and clinical evidence included; risk of bias evaluated.
  • Preclinical evidence: natural compounds mitigated VL-induced oxidative stress, inflammation, and pigmentation-related pathway activation.
  • Clinical evidence: formulations containing natural compounds improved VL-induced erythema and pigmentation outcomes; strongest clinical evidence centred on polyphenol-rich natural sources.
  • Considerable heterogeneity in irradiation protocols, outcome measures, and study designs precluded quantitative meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis only.
  • Authors identify standardised phototesting protocols, well-designed comparative clinical trials, and skin-phototype-specific research as priority gaps; note that conventional sunscreens offer limited protection in the visible spectrum, supporting interest in complementary strategies.

Skin barrier repairing and anti-aging effects evaluation of a comprehensive topical regimen incorporating niacinamide, Pro-Xylane, and panthenol after PicoWay laser: a randomized controlled study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Liu H et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · RCT (n = 88)

  • 112-day RCT; 88 Chinese females divided into test group (REGENMEDIC cream + serum: combination of Pro-Xylane, niacinamide, panthenol, and other active agents following PicoWay laser treatment) vs. control group (standard post-laser cream).
  • Skin barrier assessment over the initial 14 days (REGENMEDIC cream only): test group showed lower transepidermal water loss (TEWL), higher hydration, and more significant reduction in skin redness vs. control at 14 days.
  • Anti-aging endpoints assessed over 112 days (cream + serum combined): test group demonstrated increased skin elasticity and firmness, reduced wrinkles, and improved overall skin tone and texture vs. control.
  • Tolerability: dermatologist-rated as excellent; minimal adverse events in both groups.
  • Single-center; exclusively Chinese female sample; manufacturer involvement in study not explicitly stated but product names are proprietary (REGENMEDIC); outcome measures are predominantly instrumental/dermatologist-assessed rather than patient-reported.

Treatment of androgenetic alopecia in transgender people receiving testosterone therapy with sublingual minoxidil: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Tang GT et al. · J Am Acad Dermatol (2026) · RCT (n = 46)

  • 24-week double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; 46 transgender participants (23 per arm) on full-dose testosterone therapy with mild androgenetic alopecia; 1:1 randomisation to sublingual minoxidil 1.35 mg twice daily or placebo; followed by open-label extension with sublingual minoxidil 2.5 mg twice daily.
  • Co-primary endpoints: change in midfrontal and vertex target area hair count assessed via tattoo-directed phototrichograms.
  • At Week 24: midfrontal target area hair count improved by +15.7 hairs/cm² in the minoxidil arm (specific comparator and p-value not fully reported in available abstract).
  • Sublingual minoxidil was well tolerated; no interference with masculinization reported — an important safety signal given that 5α-reductase inhibitors (standard AGA treatment) can attenuate testosterone-driven masculinization.
  • Mild baseline AGA severity may have limited the sensitivity of global photographic and quality-of-life assessments to detect change; small sample (n = 46); single study design in a specific population.
