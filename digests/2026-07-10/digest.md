## TL;DR — Top 5 Highlights

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GLP-1 RA + SGLT2i combination in heart failure** (n = 75,833): Adding a GLP-1 receptor agonist on top of an SGLT2 inhibitor cut all-cause mortality by 38% (RR 0.62) and worsening heart failure events by 26% (RR 0.74) vs. SGLT2i alone, with no significant increase in adverse events. Moderate GRADE evidence from observational data only — RCTs urgently needed.

2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GLP-1 RA effect on kidney albumin excretion** (n = 26,088): Across 10 RCTs, GLP-1 RAs reduced urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio by −27.94% and body weight by −5.85 kg vs. placebo; effect on albuminuria appeared robust even outside type 2 diabetes, though nondiabetic CKD data remain limited.

3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Potassium citrate for bone turnover in menopause** (5 RCTs, n = 401): Supplementation significantly reduced urinary NTX (bone resorption marker) at 1 month (MD −4.05 nmol BCE/mmol creatinine, p = 0.047) vs. placebo; longer-term bone mineral density data remain sparse.

4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Resistance training in head & neck cancer during chemoradiation** (n = 50): 12-week progressive resistance training did not attenuate lean soft tissue loss (~3.4 kg in both groups) but did significantly improve functional performance (chair rise, arm curls, p < 0.05) vs. usual care — trial underpowered (target n = 72, enrolled n = 50).

5. ⭐⭐⭐ **Sleep–adipokine–metabolism bidirectional crosstalk** (narrative review): Sleep deprivation and OSA dysregulate leptin, adiponectin, IL-6, and ANGPTL4 in mechanistically distinct ways, each linking to insulin resistance and dyslipidemia; conversely, obesity worsens sleep through visceral fat, HPA-axis activation, nocturia, and neuropathic pain.

### [Combination Therapy of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i in Patients with Established Heart Failure: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Observational Studies](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40256-026-00812-w)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Tristan et al. · Am J Cardiovasc Drugs (2026) · n = 75,833 · meta-analysis

- Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 8 observational cohorts (n = 75,833) comparing GLP-1 RA + SGLT2i combination vs. SGLT2i alone in patients with pre-existing heart failure; searches conducted February 2026 across PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Scopus; quality assessed with NOS; evidence certainty with GRADE.
- Primary endpoint — all-cause mortality: RR 0.62 (95% CI 0.47–0.83; p = 0.0014) in favor of combination therapy at ~1-year follow-up.
- All-cause hospitalization: RR 0.89 (95% CI 0.84–0.96; p = 0.0012); worsening heart failure: RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.64–0.87; p = 0.0001).
- Secondary: LVEF improved by MD +3.83% (95% CI 0.44–7.22; p = 0.0397); acute kidney injury risk reduced (HR 0.70; 95% CI 0.65–0.74).
- No significant increase in hypoglycemia, GI events, pancreatitis, or UTIs with combination vs. SGLT2i alone; GRADE certainty rated moderate for all three primary endpoints; all included studies observational — residual confounding cannot be excluded; PROSPERO CRD420261320840.

### [Effect of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists on body weight and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio in patients with and without type 2 diabetes: A systematic review and meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/03000605261458641)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Huang R, Wang B · J Int Med Res (2026) · n = 26,088 · meta-analysis

- PRISMA-guided meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n = 26,088) comparing GLP-1 RAs vs. placebo or standard care; primary outcomes were change in body weight (kg) and percentage change in urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR); secondary outcome was change in eGFR; prespecified subgroup analyses by glycemic status conducted.
- Body weight reduction: MD −5.85 kg (95% CI −7.78 to −3.92) with consistent directional effect across trials despite heterogeneity.
- UACR reduction overall: MD −27.94% (95% CI −37.72 to −18.15); stratified analysis in type 2 diabetes: MD −25.70% (I² not fully reported in abstract excerpt).
- Evidence on albuminuria lowering in nondiabetic CKD identified as uncertain — the abstract notes this remains an open question; eGFR changes reported as secondary but full data not extractable from abstract.
- Risk of bias assessed with ROB 2.0; funnel plot and trim-and-fill analyses performed for small-study effects; sensitivity analyses included leave-one-out and low-risk-only subsets; GRADE certainty ratings applied.

### [Microbiota-liver axis and host transcriptomic mechanisms underlying the anti-obesity effects of Bifidobacterium animalis DPU-MWFBA in early-life overfeeding](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fm.2026.105216)
⭐⭐⭐ · Gao et al. · Food Microbiol (2026) · preclinical study

- Mouse model study using small-litter rearing (early-life overfeeding model) followed by 2-week oral intervention with Bifidobacterium animalis strain FBA-40; outcomes included body weight, adiposity, glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, hepatic histology, and gut microbiota composition.
- FBA-40 attenuated excess body weight gain and adiposity; improved glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity; reduced dyslipidemia, systemic inflammation, and hepatic lipid accumulation in treated vs. control animals — all effect sizes reported qualitatively, no numerical data in abstract.
- Intestinal barrier integrity: FBA-40 increased ZO-1 and Occludin expression; suppressed TNF-α-associated inflammatory activation per immunohistochemistry.
- Hepatic transcriptomics: FBA-40 reprogrammed PPAR signaling, linoleic acid metabolism, cholesterol metabolism, bile secretion, and arachidonic acid metabolism pathways; suppressed lipogenesis targets (Scd1, Acaca, Lpin1, SCD1); restored PPARα/EHHADH fatty acid β-oxidation and GPX1-mediated antioxidant defense.
- Entirely preclinical (murine); no human data; strain-specific findings may not generalize to other Bifidobacterium strains or human populations.

### [CDK4/6 inhibition induces a senescence-associated secretory phenotype via delayed NF-κB activation](https://doi.org/10.26508/lsa.202603790)
⭐⭐⭐ · Yeung et al. · Life Sci Alliance (2026)

- In vitro mechanistic study profiling transcriptional regulatory dynamics in response to the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib vs. the DNA-damaging agent doxorubicin, specifically examining how SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) is induced under each condition.
- NF-κB-driven SASP gene upregulation was shared across both palbociclib and doxorubicin conditions, but the onset was delayed under CDK4/6i — coinciding with slower enhancer activation and epigenetic changes (chromatin remodeling).
- ATM/ATR inhibition did not affect CDK4/6i-induced NF-κB nuclear localization, suggesting an alternative upstream activator distinct from canonical DNA-damage signaling.
- NF-κB inhibition suppressed SASP gene expression without reversing stable cell cycle arrest — pointing to SASP as a potentially separable, manipulable downstream consequence of CDK4/6i-driven senescence.
- Entirely in vitro / cell line study; no animal or human data; relevance to cancer therapy and senostatic strategies requires in vivo validation.

### [Glycolysis-driven immunosuppression in gastric cancer: Metabolic crosstalk between tumor cells and the immune microenvironment](https://doi.org/10.3892/ijo.2026.5912)
⭐⭐⭐ · Zhang et al. · Int J Oncol (2026) · review

- Narrative mechanistic review examining how tumor-intrinsic glycolytic reprogramming and lactate accumulation create an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) in gastric cancer, potentially explaining limited response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) in a subset of patients.
- Key glycolytic drivers: oncogenic signaling via HIF-1α, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, and noncoding RNA networks upregulate hexokinase 2, PFKFB3, PKM2, LDHA, and monocarboxylate transporters — establishing a high-lactate TME.
- Lactate in the TME: impairs dendritic cell differentiation and cross-priming; weakens cytotoxic T-cell and NK-cell activity; promotes M2-like macrophages and MDSCs via HCA1/GPR81-dependent signaling and histone lactylation.
- Cancer-associated fibroblasts and mesenchymal stromal cells further reinforce immunosuppression through lactate shuttling, cytokine secretion, ECM remodeling, and exosome-mediated transfer of glycolysis-promoting noncoding RNAs.
- Review only; no original data; no systematic search; primarily a mechanistic synthesis relevant to understanding ICB resistance and potential metabolic intervention targets in gastric cancer.

## Strength & Hypertrophy

### [The effect of progressive resistance training on lean soft tissue mass in head and neck cancer patients during concomitant chemoradiotherapy: the DAHANCA 31 randomized controlled trial](https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226X.2026.45545)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Lonkvist et al. · Acta Oncol (2026) · n = 50 · RCT

- 12-week parallel-group RCT (DAHANCA 31) randomizing head and neck cancer patients initiating concurrent chemoradiation (CCRT) to progressive resistance training (PRT; 36 sessions) vs. usual care (no exercise); primary endpoint: total body lean soft tissue mass (LSTM) via DXA; also assessed 1RM chest/leg press, and functional performance (30-s chair rise, 30-s arm curls, stair climb); QoL via EORTC QLQ-C30 and H&N-35.
- Pre-specified sample size: 72 patients (36/group); actual enrollment: 50 patients (25/group) — trial formally underpowered.
- At 12 weeks: PRT group lost 8.6 ± 1.0 kg body mass (10%), 3.4 ± 0.6 kg LSTM (6%), and 5.2 ± 0.8 kg fat mass (21%); CON group lost 7.4 ± 1.0 kg body mass (9%), 3.4 ± 0.6 kg LSTM (6%), and 4.0 ± 0.8 kg fat mass (17%) — LSTM loss not significantly different between groups.
- 1RM strength: decreased equally in both groups — no significant between-group difference.
- Functional performance: chair rise and arm curl performance improved significantly more in PRT vs. CON (p < 0.05) despite equivalent LSTM loss.
- Interpretation constrained by underpowering; LSTM primary endpoint negative; functional performance benefit may be clinically relevant for this patient population.

- RT + PBMT was reported (per the commented trial) to safely improve peripheral muscle strength and exercise capacity to a greater degree than RT alone.

## Supplements

### [Effects of Potassium Citrate Supplementation on Bone Turnover Markers in Postmenopausal Women: A GRADE-Assessed Systematic Review, Meta-analysis and Trial Sequential Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00223-026-01569-9)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · AlSejari et al. · Calcif Tissue Int (2026) · n = 401 · meta-analysis

- PRISMA-guided systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (n = 401 postmenopausal women) examining potassium citrate supplementation effects on bone health outcomes; databases searched: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL; GRADE certainty ratings and trial sequential analysis (TSA) conducted to assess evidence sufficiency.
- Primary finding: potassium citrate significantly reduced urinary N-terminal telopeptide (NTX — a bone resorption marker) at 1 month compared with placebo: MD = −4.05 nmol BCE/mmol creatinine (95% CI −8.05 to −0.05; p = 0.0474).
- Five RCTs; n = 401 total — a small evidence base; TSA performed to address whether current evidence is adequate to draw firm conclusions (results of TSA not fully extractable from abstract excerpt).
- Longer-term effects on bone mineral density (BMD) not reported as a significant finding in the available abstract; clinical translation of NTX reduction to fracture risk reduction not established in this dataset.
- Rationale: potassium citrate is an alkaline salt that neutralizes dietary acid load — proposed mechanism is reduction of acid-driven bone mineral dissolution, though direct mechanistic confirmation not provided in this review.

### [Genetic predisposition to serum 25 hydroxyvitamin D concentrations does not influence the risk of decreasing celiac disease in European ancestry: Evidence from meta-analysis and Mendelian randomization](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000049587)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Akbarzadeh et al. · Medicine (Baltimore) (2026) · meta-analysis + Mendelian randomization

- Combined systematic review with meta-analysis and two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) investigating whether serum 25(OH)D causally influences celiac disease (CD) risk in European ancestry populations; vitamin D GWAS: ebi-a-GCST90000618 (n = 496,946); CD GWAS: ebi-a-GCST005523 (11,812 cases, 229 controls).
- Meta-analysis (random-effects model): standardized mean difference −0.79 (95% CI −2.02 to 0.45); not statistically significant (T-statistic = −1.77; p = 0.152); Bayesian meta-analysis point estimate −0.6 (95% credible interval −13.6 to 13.2) — wide uncertainty.
- GSMR: OR = 0.988 (95% CI 0.853–1.145; p = 0.878) — no significant association; two-sample IVW MR: OR = 0.72 (95% CI 0.49–1.05; p = 0.091) — also non-significant.
- Null findings consistent across 14 MR sensitivity methods including MR-Egger, SIMEX, MR-PRESSO, Maximum Likelihood, MR-Mix, RAPS, MR-LASSO, debiased IVW, and contamination mixture; Steiger test confirmed no directional relationship; cluster analysis showed no heterogeneity patterns.
- Conclusion: genetically instrumented vitamin D levels show no causal effect on CD risk — questions the presumed direct connection between vitamin D status and celiac disease; limited to European ancestry.

- No quantitative pooled estimates; not a systematic review or meta-analysis; proposed interventions discussed (CPAP, sleep extension, circadian realignment) but no intervention efficacy data reported.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Gambin et al. · PLoS One (2026) · n = 54 (planned) · RCT protocol

- Published protocol for a monocentric, within-subject randomized crossover trial (registered ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06872593; SNCTP000006301) aiming to validate a Driving Simulation-based Maintenance of Wakefulness Test (DS-MWT) against the classical MWT; planned n = 54: 36 OSA patients (undergoing ≥7-day CPAP withdrawal [W] or continuation [C]) + 18 healthy controls.

- Statistical plan: linear mixed models, repeated-measures ANOVA, predictive modeling with cross-validation.
- Protocol only — no results available; study addresses the clinical gap between passive-condition wakefulness tests and real-world driving fitness determination; potential implications for clinical decision-making in OSA management.

### [Retinoic acid disrupts cumulus granulosa cells function via EGFR downregulation: induction of PCOS and infertility in adult female mice](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00418-026-02508-3)
⭐⭐⭐ · Said et al. · Histochem Cell Biol (2026) · n = 12 mice · preclinical

- Mouse study (n = 12 female mice, 2 groups of 6): negative control (DMSO + sunflower oil vehicle) vs. retinoic acid (RA) 10 mg/kg/day intraperitoneal for 15 days; assessed over three estrous cycles; endpoints: histopathology, immunohistochemistry (EGFR expression), and hormonal levels (FSH, LH) on day 16.
- High-dose RA (10 mg/kg/day) significantly increased FSH and reduced LH; induced polycystic ovarian morphology with increased cystic follicles and reduced antral follicles and corpus luteum.
- Immunohistochemistry: excessive RA suppressed EGFR expression in granulosa cells — proposed mechanism by which RA inhibits cumulus granulosa cell proliferation and oocyte maturation.
- Authors conclude that supraphysiological vitamin A exposure may be detrimental to female fertility via EGFR downregulation in granulosa cells.
- Critical limitations: extremely small n (6/group); suprapharmacological dose (10 mg/kg/day far exceeds clinical retinoic acid exposure); murine model only; direct translation to human fertility effects of dietary or supplemental vitamin A is not established.
