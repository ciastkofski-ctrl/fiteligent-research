Obesity & Metabolic Health

Effects of diabetes, obesity, and metabolic syndrome on alcohol-associated liver disease: A systematic review

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Peddu et al. · Hepatol Commun (2026) · n = 132,054 · meta-analysis

  • Systematic review of peer-reviewed studies through October 21, 2025, plus conference abstracts (AASLD, EASL, DDW, ACG, APASL 2023–2025); 19 studies met inclusion criteria comprising 132,054 patients with ALD.
  • Primary outcomes: incident cirrhosis, HCC, overall/cause-specific mortality, cardiovascular events, liver-related complications, and liver transplantation.
  • Diabetes was associated with increased overall mortality (aHR 3.00), liver-related mortality (aHR 3.60), HCC (HR range 1.6–21.7), and cardiovascular mortality (aHR 19.91).
  • Elevated BMI linked to higher all-cause mortality (aHR 1.16–1.58), cardiovascular mortality (aHR 3.76), HCC incidence (HR 2.0–2.9), and liver-related mortality (aHR 16.22).
  • Metabolic syndrome associated with increased overall mortality (HR 1.27–2.37) and liver-related mortality (HR 1.47–2.06); greater CMRF burden correlated with lower transplant-free survival and reduced hepatic recompensation rates.

Multiple generation distinct toxicant exposures and epigenetic transgenerational inheritance of sex-specific outcross impacts on sperm epigenetics and obesity pathology

⭐⭐⭐ · De Santos et al. · Epigenetics (2026)

  • Rat model study: three consecutive generations (F0–F2) were exposed separately to vinclozolin, a jet fuel hydrocarbon mixture, and DDT, generating a transgenerational F5 generation; F5 was then outcrossed with wild-type rats to produce maternal (MOC) and paternal (POC) F6 lineages.
  • Differential DNA methylated regions (DMRs) were compared between F6 outcross lineages and a control F3 generation (no toxicant exposure history).
  • F6 lineages showed a higher number of DMRs compared to controls; only the F6 POC generation shared overlapping DMRs with the F5 multigenerational lineage, consistent with a paternal transmission pattern.
  • Obesity pathology was assessed in F6 outcross lineages primarily as confirmatory outcome; full pathology analysis was not the primary aim of this study.
  • Both MOC and POC lineages showed some DMR overlap, suggesting both oocyte- and sperm-mediated epigenetic inheritance following ancestral toxicant exposure; all results are rodent-derived and not directly translatable to humans.

Lipedema and obesity: biological interactions, diagnostic challenges, and principles of interdisciplinary management — a narrative review

⭐⭐⭐ · Scherkamp et al. · Adipocyte (2026) · review

  • Narrative review synthesizing PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library literature; no formal systematic screening or risk-of-bias assessment was performed; literature selected purposively for predefined clinical and mechanistic domains.
  • Target population: predominantly adult women with suspected or confirmed lipedema, including those with coexisting overweight or obesity.
  • Obesity is conceptualized as an adiposity-based chronic disease; obesity can impair lymphatic morphology and function, while lymphatic dysfunction may promote subcutaneous adipose-tissue expansion and fibrosis — posited as a bidirectional relationship.
  • Lipedema and obesity frequently coexist and may aggravate one another despite being classically treated as differential diagnoses.
  • The proposed staged interdisciplinary management framework is intended to support clinical reasoning; authors explicitly note it does not constitute a formal guideline, and no quantitative outcomes or effect sizes are reported.

Aging and hypnosis

⭐⭐⭐ · Casula CC · Am J Clin Hypn (2026) · review

  • Narrative article exploring the application of hypnosis to the aging process, framed as an extension of existing hypnotherapy literature on female-specific health issues (eating disorders, hormonal transitions).
  • Scope extends beyond menopause to position aging broadly as a phase of life that can be approached with psychological flexibility and creativity.
  • No clinical trial data, effect sizes, or patient cohorts are reported; no formal methodology for literature synthesis is described.
  • Conceptual framework only; no quantitative evidence for hypnosis improving longevity outcomes is presented in this article.

Bioinformatics analysis reveals shared gene signatures and molecular mechanisms between periodontitis and rheumatoid arthritis in the context of aging

⭐⭐⭐ · Xiao et al. · Acta Odontol Scand (2026)

  • Gene expression profiles for periodontitis (PD) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database; differentially expressed genes were intersected with aging-related genes from GeneCards, yielding 191 aging-related crosstalk genes.
  • Protein-protein interaction network analysis and two machine learning algorithms identified 5 hub genes: RAC2, CSF2RB, IL2RG, ENTPD1, and IL10RA; all showed significant overexpression in both PD and RA tissues with "excellent" diagnostic value (specific AUC values not reported in abstract).
  • Functional enrichment (GO/KEGG) identified enrichment in immune-inflammatory pathways; CIBERSORT immune infiltration analysis revealed coordinated dysregulation of plasma cells and γδ T cells in both diseases.
  • Study design is bioinformatic/computational; no clinical outcomes or interventional data; external dataset validation was performed but sample sizes not specified in abstract.
  • Findings provide candidate biomarkers (RAC2, CSF2RB, IL2RG, ENTPD1, IL10RA) and a theoretical mechanistic basis linking immune-inflammatory aging to both PD and RA; prospective clinical validation not yet conducted.

A bacterial enzyme enhances both energy metabolism and health across the life span of C. elegans and mice

⭐⭐⭐ · Liu et al. · Sci Adv (2026)

  • Bacterial-derived lipoic acid protein ligase A (LplA) introduced as an orthogonal molecular tool to enhance energy metabolism; tested in C. elegans and mice.
  • Rationale: enhanced energy metabolism is often associated with increased oxidative damage risk; LplA is proposed to resolve this paradox by simultaneously fostering a low oxidative stress environment.
  • Abstract is truncated in the available source; specific quantitative outcomes (lifespan extension magnitude, metabolic parameter changes, group sizes) are not discernible from the provided text.
  • Study represents a mechanistic proof-of-concept in model organisms; translation to humans is speculative at this stage.
  • Published in Science Advances (2026-08-21); full results require access to the complete manuscript.

Strength & Hypertrophy

Associations between osteoarthritis and comorbidities before and after diagnosis across four European primary care databases: a multinational case-control and cohort study

⭐⭐⭐ · Swain et al. · Lancet Reg Health Eur (2026) · n = 845,373 OA cases / 2,556,243 controls

  • Multinational ComOA study combining case-control (retrospective) and cohort (prospective) designs using primary care records from UK, Netherlands, Sweden, and Spain; patients aged ≥18 years; incident OA cases matched by age and sex to controls without OA.
  • Retrospectively, 10 of 33 comorbidities showed congruent (all-country directionally consistent) associations with OA, including fibromyalgia (aOR 1.93; 95% CI 1.49–2.49), polymyalgia rheumatica (aOR 1.44; 95% CI 1.31–1.58), and chronic back pain (aOR 1.42; 95% CI 1.32–1.53).
  • Prospectively, 3 of 8 studied conditions showed congruent associations after OA diagnosis: depression (aHR 1.11; 95% CI 1.07–1.16), osteoporosis (aHR 1.05; 95% CI 1.01–1.09), and hypertension (aHR 1.05; 95% CI 1.02–1.09).
  • Additional contingency observed retrospectively and prospectively for sleep problems, migraine, asthma, eczema, and benign prostatic hypertrophy.
  • Percentage of women varied across databases (41.7% in Spain to 64.0% in Netherlands); mean age at OA diagnosis ranged 59.8–65.8 years; cross-national congruency strengthens causal inference but mechanisms remain unexplained.

Severe mitochondrial cardiomyopathy with m.3303C>T variant in the mitochondrial tRNA(Leu(UUR)) gene: A study of large family analysis

⭐⭐⭐ · Yatsuga S · Mol Genet Metab Rep (2026)

  • Case series of a Japanese family carrying the m.3303C>T variant in the mitochondrial tRNALeu(UUR) gene across three generations; 7 relatives screened for variant heteroplasmy levels.
  • Proband: male newborn with severe hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, generalized hypotonia, and profound lactic acidosis (43.9 mmol/L); died at 11 days of age from multiorgan failure; autopsy confirmed diffuse cardiac hypertrophy and abnormal mitochondria.
  • Genetic analysis showed 98% heteroplasmy in the proband across multiple tissues; family members with heteroplasmy <90% were asymptomatic; one maternal uncle with 100% muscle heteroplasmy died at age 7 from mitochondrial myopathy.
  • Demonstrates an exceptionally high threshold effect: severe phenotype manifested only when heteroplasmy exceeded 90%; variant loads ranged 6%–100% across family members.
  • Single-family case series; findings specific to this rare variant and this pedigree; limited generalizability.

Note: Study classified under Strength & Hypertrophy by source theme_guess; content is primarily mitochondrial/cardiac genetics.

Coronary CT angiography-derived perivascular fat attenuation index as a potential marker for heart failure in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

⭐⭐⭐ · Peng et al. · Br J Radiol (2026) · n = 75 HCM patients + 75 matched controls

  • Retrospective study; 75 patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) and 75 age/sex-matched controls without structural heart disease; all underwent coronary CT angiography (CCTA) and cardiac MRI.
  • Perivascular fat attenuation index (FAI) measured around proximal RCA, LAD, and LCX: LAD-FAI (−75.49 ± 9.04 vs. −83.25 ± 8.97 HU), LCX-FAI (−71.96 ± 9.62 vs. −79.48 ± 8.51 HU), RCA-FAI (−77.57 ± 10.25 vs. −83.20 ± 8.98 HU); all p < 0.001 vs. controls.
  • LAD-FAI showed "modest but significant" correlations with LV mass index and global strain parameters (specific r-values not reported in abstract).
  • Multivariable logistic regression: LAD-FAI independently associated with heart failure (OR 1.40; 95% CI 1.12–1.99; p = 0.018) after adjustment for CMR parameters.
  • Single-center retrospective design; small sample (n=150 total); authors note external validation in larger prospective multicenter cohorts is required before clinical adoption.

Synergistic effects of synbiotic, protein supplementation, and resistance training on inflammation, oxidative stress, and muscle strength in older adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus: a RCT triple-blinded study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Bastos et al. · Eur J Nutr (2026) · n = 40 (completers) · RCT

  • Triple-blinded RCT; men aged ≥65 years with T2DM; 51 enrolled, 40 completed (Control n=13, Protein [P] n=13, Synbiotic+Protein [SP] n=14); all groups performed resistance training (RT).
  • All three groups showed improvements in physical performance and enhanced redox balance (higher uric acid as endogenous antioxidant, lower plasma iron concentrations).
  • Protein group demonstrated greater improvements in muscle strength, reduced insulin resistance, and lower oxidative damage associated with heme-iron species compared to Control; specific effect sizes not reported in abstract.
  • Pro-inflammatory cytokines IFN-γ and IL-6 were elevated in the Protein group; authors attribute this to physiological exercise-induced metabolic adaptation rather than pathological inflammation.
  • Adding synbiotics (SP group) provided no incremental benefit beyond protein + RT alone for any reported outcome; all groups showed increased lactate dehydrogenase and creatine kinase activity (muscle damage markers).

Preventive effect of Saccharomyces boulardii combined with Bifidobacterium quadruple viable preparation on antibiotic-associated diarrhea in infants and young children: a prospective randomized controlled study

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Chang et al. · Zhongguo Dang Dai Er Ke Za Zhi (2026) · RCT

  • Prospective randomized parallel-controlled trial; hospitalized children aged 1–36 months receiving antibiotics; enrolled October 2023–June 2025; four arms (control + three probiotic combinations, exact group sizes not fully reported in available abstract excerpt).
  • Primary outcome: incidence of antibiotic-associated diarrhea (AAD); combined group (Saccharomyces boulardii + Bifidobacterium quadruple viable preparation) showed AAD incidence of 7% vs. higher rates in comparator groups (specific comparator incidence values truncated in abstract).
  • Abstract is partially truncated in the source; full quantitative comparisons between all four arms and statistical parameters (95% CIs, p-values) are not available from the provided text.
  • Published in a Chinese-language journal; results may reflect local antibiotic prescribing practices and patient population characteristics.
  • Study suggests combination probiotic prophylaxis may reduce AAD in young children; data should be interpreted with caution given incomplete abstract.

Dose-response association between perinatal 25(OH)D status and postpartum depression

⭐⭐⭐ · Yang et al. · J Psychosom Obstet Gynaecol (2026) · n = 326

  • Observational cohort of 326 perinatal women; PPD assessed within 6 weeks postpartum using EPDS; serum 25(OH)D measured at 24–48 hours after delivery.
  • PPD incidence in this cohort: 23.31%; the PPD group had lower 25(OH)D levels, negatively correlated with EPDS scores.
  • Each 1 ng/mL increase in 25(OH)D associated with 25% reduction in PPD risk (OR = 0.75; 95% CI 0.68–0.82; p-value not explicitly stated in abstract excerpt but implied as significant given CI excludes 1.0).
  • Relationship described as "linearly and negatively correlated"; 25(OH)D showed "good screening utility" (specific AUC not reported in abstract excerpt).
  • Exogenous vitamin D supplementation appeared to modify the protective effect; study design is observational — causality cannot be established; confounding by unmeasured variables (e.g., sun exposure, diet, pre-existing depression) not fully addressable.

Sleep & Recovery

Effects of traditional Chinese medicine directional drug penetration therapy on gastrointestinal function, gut microbiota, and sleep quality in postoperative ovarian cancer patients: a randomized controlled trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Wu & Wang · Support Care Cancer (2026) · n = 112 · RCT

  • RCT; 112 postoperative ovarian cancer patients randomly assigned to observation group (conventional rehabilitation + TCM directional drug penetration therapy until first bowel movement; n=56) vs. control group (conventional rehabilitation only; n=56).
  • Observation group showed significantly shorter recovery times to first bowel sounds, anal flatus, and defecation (p < 0.05 for all); specific time differences not reported in abstract.
  • LEfSe analysis: dominant bacteria in observation group — Veillonellales-Selenomonadales, Megasphaera, Corynebacterium; control group dominated by Gardnerella; KEGG functional prediction showed gene sequence abundance of phosphotransferase system (PTS) pathway significantly lower in observation group.
  • Post-intervention PSQI total score, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, and sleep disturbance scores all significantly lower (improved) in observation group vs. control (p < 0.05); absolute score differences not provided in abstract.
  • Highly specific population (post-surgical ovarian cancer); intervention (TCM directional drug penetration) is not well-characterized in the abstract; generalizability to non-surgical or non-oncologic populations is limited.

The association between pubertal timing and diurnal cortisol activity in middle adolescence

⭐⭐⭐ · Roberts & Flouri · Stress (2026) · n = 914

  • Prospective cohort study using data from the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children (ALSPAC); n=914 (54.70% female); pubertal timing measured across ages 8–15.5 years (menarche for girls; voice breaking for boys); HPA axis activity measured by salivary cortisol at age 15.5 years.
  • Outcomes assessed: diurnal cortisol slope, cortisol awakening response (CAR), total morning cortisol, and total daily cortisol output at age 15.5 years.
  • In girls, earlier menarche was associated with higher (logged) CAR (b = −0.003; 95% CI = −0.007, −0.0004); association was significant only after adjustment for confounders, particularly ethnicity.
  • No associations between pubertal timing and any cortisol measure were observed in boys.
  • Findings suggest sex-specific HPA axis programming associated with pubertal timing; effect size is small; practical clinical significance of the cortisol difference is unclear.

Clinical insights into catathrenia: A real-world analysis from a tertiary sleep center

⭐⭐⭐ · Lopes et al. · Pulmonology (2026) · n = 10 · cohort

  • Retrospective observational study at Serviço de Medicina do Sono de Coimbra; 10 adult patients diagnosed with catathrenia by attended overnight polysomnography (PSG) with synchronized audio-video recording.
  • Median age 46 years (range 27–78); 70% female; common comorbidities included obesity (specific prevalence not fully reported in available abstract excerpt).
  • Catathrenia events defined as deep inhalation followed by prolonged exhalation with monotonous groaning; study confirms REM-sleep predominance as a key diagnostic PSG feature.
  • Catathrenia frequently misdiagnosed as OSA, CSA, or parasomnia; individualized treatment often involving PAP therapy reported to improve symptoms.
  • Extremely small sample (n=10); single-center; retrospective; findings are descriptive and not suitable for quantitative generalization; no control group.

Skin & Hair Health

Aging Like Milk or Fine Wine: Content Analysis of Reactions to One's Future Self on TikTok

⭐⭐⭐ · Ng & Indran · JMIR Aging (2026)

  • Content analysis of TikTok videos using the AI "aged" filter; initial dataset of 2,682 publicly available videos (hashtags #AgedFilter, #OldFilter, #AgeFilter; combined >2,068,100,000 views at time of analysis); after exclusion criteria, 681 videos analyzed (total likes: 34,382,236; favorites: 1,204,741; comments: 211,006).
  • Both deductive and inductive coding approaches used; 7 themes identified: anxiety about aging (50%; n=342 videos), pursuit of youthfulness (14%; n=95), aging with confidence (12%; n=84), beauty of aging (8%; n=54), privilege of aging (7%; n=48), generational connections (6%; n=43), inevitability of aging (2%; n=15).
  • Several dermatologists cited in the literature as having attested to the accuracy of the AI filter in simulating aging effects; no formal clinical validation of filter accuracy was performed in this study.
  • Authors note ageist beliefs may be prevalent among users; majority framing of aged appearance was anxiety-driven or youth-seeking rather than positive.
  • Study is observational social media content analysis; no health outcome data; self-selected sample of TikTok creators; findings reflect platform-specific culture and cannot be generalized to broader population attitudes toward aging.

Patient-reported satisfaction and experience of a novel 40.68 MHz radiofrequency therapy: a retrospective real-world study

⭐⭐⭐ · Shao et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · n = 76

  • Retrospective cross-sectional survey study; 76 patients received a single full-face treatment with a 40.68 MHz monopolar radiofrequency (RF) device; structured questionnaire administered at 3-month follow-up.
  • Overall satisfaction: 86.84% (95% CI 77.5%–92.7%) rated "satisfied" or "very satisfied"; greatest self-reported improvements in skin tightening/lifting and facial contour, with jawline identified as the most improved area by 50% (38/76) of patients.
  • Tolerability: 88.16% (95% CI 79.2%–93.6%) reported intraoperative pain ≤3 on a 10-point VAS; 94.74% (95% CI 87.6%–97.8%) reported no significant side effects; 80.26% had ≤1 day of downtime; 98.68% (95% CI 93.5%–99.8%) expressed willingness to undergo repeat treatment.
  • Physician assessment at 3 months showed improvement in 92.11% (95% CI 83.6%–96.3%) of patients, consistent with patient-reported improvement rate of 94.74% (72/76).
  • No control group; retrospective design; patient-reported outcomes subject to recall and satisfaction bias; no device-related adverse events reported, but absence of comparator limits efficacy conclusions.

Objective and subjective retrospective evaluation of XERF, a novel single-shot dual-frequency non-invasive monopolar radiofrequency

⭐⭐⭐ · Erlich et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · n = 16

  • Retrospective study; 16 adult female patients who underwent a single XERF treatment session (Cynosure-Lutronic, South Korea); a dual-frequency monopolar RF device delivering 6.78 MHz and 2 MHz energy sequentially in a single pulse.
  • Objective outcomes assessed via 3D stereophotogrammetry, periorbital morphometric analysis (n=15), and computerized skin-quality evaluation at baseline, immediately post-treatment, and at 1- and 3-month follow-up.
  • Volumetric redistribution observed: lateral volume augmentation and medial volume reduction; progressive elevation of eyebrows and upper eyelids at 1 and 3 months; skin-quality improvements in wrinkle severity, skin evenness, pore size, and oiliness; no significant changes in pigmentation.
  • Subjective outcomes (FACE-Q instruments, VAS for perceived age and pain, PGAIS) consistent with objective findings; reduction in perceived age reported; treatment well tolerated; only mild/transient adverse events documented.
  • Very small sample (n=16); all female; single-center; retrospective; no control group; authors explicitly classify findings as preliminary requiring confirmation in larger prospective controlled studies.
