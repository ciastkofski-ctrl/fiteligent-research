## Obesity & Metabolic Health

### [Effectiveness and Safety of Semaglutide in Type 1 Diabetes: A Danish Nationwide Cohort Study (2018–2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101716)
⭐⭐⭐ · Würtz Yazdanfard et al. · Lancet Reg Health Eur (2026) · Nationwide cohort study

- Danish registry study (2018–2024) identifying individuals with T1D initiating semaglutide, matched 1:4 to unexposed T1D controls using exposure density matching, followed for up to two years.
- 879 semaglutide initiators identified: MDI users n=622, insulin pump users n=257; median semaglutide dose redeemed during follow-up was 1.0 mg.
- HbA1c decreased by 5.7 mmol/mol (0.52%) (95% CI: 5.0–6.3) in the first six months in semaglutide users, remained stable thereafter; control group HbA1c was unchanged.
- No increased hospitalization rate for hypoglycemia vs. controls (HR 0.64; 95% CI: 0.35–1.19) and no increased DKA rate (HR 0.73; 95% CI: 0.34–1.57).
- Cumulative probability of adherence at one year was 50% (95% CI: 47–54%); no difference in HbA1c reduction between MDI and insulin pump users (p=0.42).

### [Preoperative Weight Loss Intervention With GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Is Associated With Lower Complication Rates Following Primary Total Knee Arthroplasty Than Preoperative Bariatric Surgery](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artd.2026.102033)

- Database study comparing obese patients (BMI ≥35 kg/m²) undergoing primary TKA stratified by preoperative intervention: bariatric surgery (BS), GLP-1 RA, or no weight-loss intervention within 1 year prior to surgery.
- Propensity score matching was performed to balance baseline characteristics; subgroup analyses conducted for BMI 35–40 kg/m².
- GLP-1 RA preoperative use was associated with lower short- and long-term complication rates compared to both bariatric surgery and no weight-loss intervention (specific ORs/HRs not extractable from available abstract text).
- Authors describe a "protective safety profile" for GLP-1 RAs compared to no weight-loss intervention in the matched BMI ≥35 kg/m² cohort.
- Retrospective design and database-derived complication coding are key limitations; causal inference is not possible.

### [Evaluation of the Safety Profile of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: A Focus on Thyroid Cancer-Related Adverse Events Using the European Pharmacovigilance Database](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43440-026-00864-4)
⭐⭐⭐ · Anatriello et al. · Pharmacol Rep (2026) · Pharmacovigilance/disproportionality analysis

- Analyzed individual case safety reports (ICSRs) from EudraVigilance (1 January 2022 – 26 September 2024) for semaglutide, liraglutide, exenatide, lixisenatide, dulaglutide, and tirzepatide as suspected drugs; total 34,956 ICSRs included.
- Most reported system organ classes: gastrointestinal disorders, general disorders and administration site conditions, and injury/poisoning/procedural complications; majority of AEs in adult and elderly female patients.
- Disproportionality analysis (reporting odds ratio): semaglutide had a lower probability of reporting thyroid cancer-related AEs than tirzepatide (ROR = 0.54, 95% CI 0.37–0.81, p<0.05).
- Sensitivity analysis stratified by therapeutic indication revealed no significant signals across stratified groups.
- Pharmacovigilance databases cannot establish causality; reporting bias, indication confounding, and differential prescribing patterns are key limitations; authors recommend further studies.

## Longevity

### [New Regulators of Parathyroid Hormone Secretion](https://doi.org/10.1097/MNH.0000000000001190)
⭐⭐⭐ · Naveh-Many et al. · Curr Opin Nephrol Hypertens (2026) · Narrative review

- Review synthesizing experimental and clinical evidence on leptin and mTORC1 as newly identified regulators of parathyroid hormone (PTH) secretion and parathyroid gland biology, with focus on chronic kidney disease-associated secondary hyperparathyroidism (CKD-SHP).
- Leptin directly stimulates PTH secretion, at least partly by downregulating calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) signaling, establishing a leptin-PTH axis.
- In experimental CKD models, uremia activates parathyroid mTORC1, driving cellular hyperplasia and PTH overproduction; rapamycin treatment both prevents and reverses these changes.
- Genetic mouse models: parathyroid-specific mTOR deletion impairs gland integrity and adaptive PTH responses; constitutive mTORC1 activation (via Tsc1 deletion) induces autonomous gland enlargement and impaired secretory capacity.
- Clinical observation: kidney transplant recipients on mTOR inhibitors exhibit lower PTH levels and reduced SHP incidence vs. those on other immunosuppressants — an association, not an RCT finding.

### [Multicellular Senescence Impairs Skeletal Muscle Recovery Following Disuse in Aging](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed5255)
⭐⭐⭐ · Bourrant et al. · Sci Adv (2026) · Animal (mouse) study using flow cytometry + scRNAseq

- Young and aged mice underwent 14 days of hindlimb unloading followed by 7 or 14 days of reloading; flow cytometry coupled with single-cell RNA sequencing used to characterize senescent cell identity and transcriptional landscape during recovery.
- Aged mice showed smaller myofibers and abnormal muscle macrophage dynamics at recovery, with greater collagen content compared to young mice.
- Elevated senescence markers (p21 and γH2AX) and increased SPiDER-β-Gal staining were found in aged muscle at recovery timepoints.
- Senescent cells were identified across multiple cell types (multicellular senescence), with transcriptional landscape suggesting impaired immune-progenitor cell crosstalk as a mechanism for delayed remodeling.
- Findings are in murine models only; translational relevance to human disuse-recovery scenarios (e.g., bed rest, immobilization) requires further investigation.

### [Intermittent Fasting Attenuates Cognitive Decline in D-Galactose-Induced Aging Rats in Association with β-Hydroxybutyrate and PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β Signaling](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11064-026-04804-9)
⭐⭐⭐ · Lin et al. · Neurochem Res (2026) · Animal (rat) study

- 46 male Sprague-Dawley rats (2 months old) assigned to five groups: Control, D-galactose-induced aging (D-gal), D-gal + IF, D-gal + IF + LY294002 (PI3K inhibitor), D-gal + IF + Solvent; IF administered for 6 weeks concurrently with D-gal treatment.
- IF improved recognition memory (novel object recognition test) and spatial learning/memory (Morris water maze), preserved hippocampal neuronal morphology, and increased the proportion of morphologically normal CA1 neurons vs. D-gal alone.
- IF elevated serum β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) levels, reduced neuroinflammation and tau phosphorylation, and restored BDNF expression; these improvements were attenuated by LY294002, implicating PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β pathway involvement.
- RNA-seq transcriptomic analysis revealed suppression of senescence-related genes and modulation of neuroprotective pathways in IF-treated rats.
- D-galactose model is a surrogate for aging, not true aging; male animals only; PI3K inhibitor LY294002 has off-target effects; causal role of ketone metabolism acknowledged by authors as requiring further validation.

## Strength & Hypertrophy

### [Change in Skeletal Muscle Mass During Systemic Cancer Treatment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226X.2026.45726)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Svendsen et al. · Acta Oncol (2026) · Systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42022308388)

- Systematic search of PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science through April 2025; 78 longitudinal studies included (n=10,502; 52% male; median age 64 years, IQR 34–77); studies reported skeletal muscle mass (SMM) during chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy (±targeted therapy).
- Meta-analysis across all cancer types: systemic treatment associated with SMM decline (SMC = −0.24, 95% CI −0.29 to −0.20; I²=92%), corresponding to approximately −5% over a median assessment interval of 90 days (IQR: 71–129 days).
- Declines were most pronounced during chemotherapy (±targeted therapy) compared to immunotherapy-based regimens.
- Heterogeneity was substantial (I²=92%), limiting the interpretation of a single pooled estimate; random-effects model with Hartung-Knapp adjustment and restricted maximum-likelihood estimator used.
- Risk of bias assessed using the NIH Quality Assessment Tool for Observational Cohort and Cross-Sectional Studies; authors call for prospective studies with standardized measurement methods.

### [Muscle Sympathetic Nerve Activity in COPD: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1183/16000617.0267-2025)
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Duiker et al. · Eur Respir Rev (2026) · Systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251044931)

- Eight databases searched from inception to 15 May 2025; 11 studies met inclusion criteria (171 COPD participants, 105 controls); COPD defined by spirometry; MSNA measured by microneurography at rest.
- COPD associated with markedly higher resting MSNA burst frequency vs. healthy controls: +18.5 bursts/min (95% CI 9.4–27.7).
- Elevated MSNA occurred despite normal blood pressure in COPD participants, indicating sympathetic overactivity independent of blood pressure regulation.
- Authors propose chronic sympathetic excitation as a likely contributor to COPD-related cardiovascular morbidity and exercise intolerance.
- Small total sample (n=276 across 11 studies); findings limited to resting MSNA; data on short-term effects of COPD interventions on MSNA reported but not fully extractable from available abstract.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Felder et al. · JMIR Res Protoc (2026) · RCT protocol

- Fully remote, 2-arm parallel-group RCT (PRISM study) targeting ≥443 pregnant participants with insomnia disorder, randomized 1:1 to digital CBT-I (SleepioRx, 6 interactive modules of 5–15 min each) or sleep hygiene education (SHE, 6 weekly digital handouts).
- Primary outcome: depression incidence measured using the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5 (SCID-5-RV) through 12 months postpartum; assessments at baseline, 5 weeks, 10 weeks, 36 weeks' gestation, and 3, 6, 9, 12 months postpartum.

- CBT-I arm (SleepioRx) incorporates sleep consolidation, stimulus control, cognitive therapy, and relaxation techniques; SHE arm serves as a credible active comparator.
- Data collection began November 2022, projected to end December 2026; this publication is a protocol paper — no efficacy results yet available.

### [Individualized Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy Improves Anxiety, Depression, Sleep Quality, Hopelessness, and Disease Severity in Fibromyalgia Syndrome: A Single-Blind Randomized Controlled Trial](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00296-026-06146-9)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Kocyigit et al. · Rheumatol Int (2026) · Single-blind RCT · n = 70 randomized (57 completers)

- 70 patients meeting 2016 ACR fibromyalgia diagnostic criteria randomized 1:1 at a tertiary physical therapy and rehabilitation clinic; intervention group (n=35) received 10-week individualized CBT by a psychologist plus routine treatment; control group (n=35) received routine treatment only; 57 completed the study (23 intervention, 34 control).
- Primary outcomes assessed pre- and post-intervention: HADS-A (anxiety), HADS-D (depression), Jenkins Sleep Scale (JSS), Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ), and Beck Hopelessness Scale (BHS).
- Intervention group: HADS-A median 13.00→10.00 (p<0.001); FIQ median 71.80→51.52 (p<0.001); BHS median 7.00→4.00 (p<0.001); significant improvements on all scales.
- Control group: no significant changes on any scale (p>0.05); no significant baseline differences between groups (p>0.05).
- Limitations: single-blind design (patient blinding not feasible for CBT); high dropout asymmetry (12 intervention vs. 1 control); single tertiary center; no long-term follow-up reported.

### [The Associations of Sleep Parameters With Sexual Quality of Life Among Postmenopausal Women](https://doi.org/10.1080/09513590.2026.2679796)
⭐⭐⭐ · Koc Kulaksiz & Yoldemir · Gynecol Endocrinol (2026) · Cross-sectional study · n = 303

- 303 postmenopausal women attending gynecology and menopause clinics completed the Sexual Quality of Life Questionnaire–Female (SQoL-F) and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).
- 113 women (37.3%) had good sexual quality of life (SQoL-F >84); sexual and relationship satisfaction were higher among women aged 40–49 years; other SQoL-F domains showed similar scores across age groups.
- Spearman correlations between SQoL-F domains and PSQI domains were negligible to low: rho values ranged from −0.181 to 0.043 (95% CI values spanning approximately −0.281 to 0.173 depending on domain pair).
- SQoL-F and PSQI scores did not differ by time since menopause; demographic characteristics did not differ between good and poor sleep quality groups.
- Correlations are unadjusted; cross-sectional design; single-center university hospital; potential selection bias from clinic-attending population.

### [Therapeutic Efficacy and Safety of Recombinant Botulinum Toxin Type A for Moderate to Severe Glabellar Lines: A Randomized, Controlled, Double-blind, Phase III Trial in Chinese Adults](https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjag042)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Wu et al. · Aesthet Surg J (2026) · Double-blind Phase III RCT · n = 529

- Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle- and active-controlled Phase III trial; 529 adults aged 18–65 with moderate-to-severe glabellar lines randomized 4:2:1 to YY001 (recombinant BoNT/A, 20 U), Botox® (20 U), or vehicle (single injection).
- Primary endpoint — composite response rate (CRR) at Week 4 (≥2-grade improvement by both investigator and participant): YY001 86.7% vs. Botox® 66.7% (p<0.0001) vs. vehicle 1.3% (p<0.0001).
- YY001 showed faster onset and longer median duration of effect (97 days) vs. Botox®; participant satisfaction rated highly.
- Adverse events were mild and comparable across all groups; only one case of anti-drug antibody observed; no neutralizing antibodies detected.
- Limitations: 12-week follow-up period; ethnically homogeneous population (Chinese adults); single injection design; longer-term immunogenicity data not yet available.

### [Efficacy and Safety of a Poly-L-Lactic Acid Filler for Temporal Augmentation: A Randomized, No-treatment Control, Evaluator-blinded, Multicenter Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjag013)
⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Chang et al. · Aesthet Surg J (2026) · Evaluator-blinded RCT · n = 174

- Randomized, no-treatment control, evaluator-blinded, multicenter trial; 174 participants with moderate-to-severe temple hollowing randomized 2:1 to PLLA treatment (n≈116) or no treatment (n≈58); treatment group received 2–3 sessions of bilateral temporal PLLA injections with 12-month follow-up; control group received no treatment within 6 months of randomization.
- Primary efficacy endpoint at Month 6: 96.5% of treatment participants achieved ≥1-grade improvement on the Allergan Temple Hollowing Scale (ATHS) vs. 0% in controls (p<0.0001).
- Significant improvements sustained across all secondary endpoints at all follow-up visits through 12 months: ATHS rates (blinded evaluators and injection investigators), GAIS improvement rates, and 3D volumetric measurements of temporal region.
- No therapy-related adverse events reported; PLLA described as safe and well tolerated; vital signs and laboratory tests monitored.
- No active comparator (hyaluronic acid or fat grafting); blinding of participants and investigators to treatment not possible; no standardized injection technique across centers specified in abstract.
