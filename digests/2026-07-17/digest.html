Obesity & Metabolic Health

Ecological Restructuring of the Nonbacterial Fecal Microbiome in Obesity Across Human Cohorts

⭐⭐⭐ · Gallardo-Nuell et al. · Gut Microbes (2026) · multicohort cross-sectional analysis

  • Multicohort analysis of three independent human cohorts examining nonbacterial gut microbiome components (viruses, archaea, eukaryotic microorganisms) in obesity — a domain historically underrepresented in gut microbiome research.
  • Compositional analyses adjusted for key covariates and network-based approaches were used to identify multikingdom alterations consistently associated with obesity status.
  • Individuals without obesity showed a reproducible enrichment of methanogenic archaea (specific taxon name truncated in available abstract text).
  • Study did not report sample sizes or specific effect estimates in the available abstract; full results require access to the complete publication.
  • Represents a methodological contribution by applying cross-kingdom compositional frameworks rather than focusing exclusively on bacteria; clinical implications remain preliminary pending replication.

Experiences and Perceptions of Pharmacological Treatment of Obesity Among Primary Health Care Physicians in Sweden

⭐⭐⭐ · Hellgren et al. · Scand J Prim Health Care (2026) · n = 11 · qualitative (in-depth interviews)

  • Qualitative content analysis of 11 in-depth interviews with primary care physicians (PCPs) in regional and private health centers in Skåne, Sweden.
  • Overarching theme identified: pharmacological treatment of obesity was perceived as useful for initiating patient discussions and supporting weight loss initiation.
  • PCPs reported four principal challenges: complexity of obesity as a condition; lack of consensus on classification and treatment targets; unclear guidance on treatment duration; and economic/reimbursement barriers for anti-obesity medications (AOMs).
  • High BMI accounts for approximately 7% of total mortality in Sweden per the study's background framing.
  • Small purposive sample from a single Swedish region; findings are not statistically generalizable but provide hypothesis-generating qualitative data for guideline development.

Obese and Overweight Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Are Not Treated Optimally — Is It Time to Reconsider Clinical Practice?

⭐⭐⭐ · Milardović et al. · Expert Rev Clin Pharmacol (2026-07-17) · narrative review

  • PubMed narrative review (search up to August 2025) examining the rationale for prioritizing weight reduction as a primary treatment strategy in overweight/obese T2DM patients.
  • Current T2DM pharmacotherapy guidelines assess cardiovascular (CV) risk but do not consistently place obesity reduction at the center of the treatment algorithm, according to the authors' framing.
  • Review notes that obesity-targeting agents may require higher doses in T2DM management than currently recommended for glycemic control alone.
  • Identified barriers to weight-centric treatment: high drug costs, absent or limited reimbursement policies, and drug side-effect profiles that reduce adherence.
  • Authors call for a multidisciplinary approach involving clinicians, health systems, and policymakers to address health inequity and disparities in medication access; no meta-analytic effect sizes reported.

Longevity

From Cell to Disease: Regulatory Networks and Mechanisms of Super-Enhancers in Aging

⭐⭐⭐ · Wang et al. · Mol Med Rep (2026-09-01) · narrative review

  • Narrative review synthesizing the past decade of super-enhancer (SE) research in the context of aging biology and age-related disease.
  • Super-enhancers are cis-regulatory elements with strong transcriptional activation capacity, bound by dense clusters of key transcription factors and cofactors; they are notably activated in response to aging-related stimuli.
  • Review covers two mechanistic angles: SE contributions to cellular senescence via DNA damage responses and mitochondrial dysfunction — both established hallmarks of aging.
  • Pathogenic SE dysregulation is discussed in two specific age-related disorders: atherosclerosis and type II diabetes.
  • No primary data; no effect sizes reported; authors identify SEs as candidate therapeutic targets and acknowledge significant challenges in clinical translation.

Targeting Alveolar Type II Cell Dysfunction in Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis: Molecular Mechanisms and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

⭐⭐⭐ · Xie et al. · Int J Mol Med (2026-09-01) · narrative review

  • Narrative review of alveolar type II (AT2) cell biology in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), a fatal progressive interstitial lung disease characterized by fibroblast proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition leading to respiratory failure.
  • AT2 cells are identified as facultative stem cells of the distal lung; the review maps regulatory pathways governing AT2 proliferation and differentiation.
  • Current approved antifibrotic agents (e.g., nintedanib, pirfenidone) slow progression by inhibiting fibroblast pathways but do not halt or reverse underlying pathology; this gap is the motivation for the review.
  • Two pathogenic drivers highlighted as key targets: cellular senescence and mechanical tension on the alveolar epithelium.
  • No primary data or quantitative effect estimates; clinical translation of AT2-targeting strategies faces unspecified limitations acknowledged by the authors.

Strength & Hypertrophy

Comparative Effects of Unilateral Versus Bilateral Training on Performance Adaptations: A Systematic Review and Multilevel Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Xin et al. · BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil (2026-07-16) · meta-analysis of 24 RCTs

  • Systematic search across PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Embase, and EBSCO; 24 RCTs included; three-level meta-analysis under random-effects models using Hedges' g as effect size metric.
  • Unilateral training significantly outperformed bilateral training for unilateral strength: g = 0.42 (95% CI 0.23–0.60, p < 0.001) and unilateral jump performance: g = 0.35 (95% CI 0.14–0.56, p = 0.002).
  • Bilateral training showed a small but significant advantage for bilateral strength: g = −0.28 (95% CI −0.54 to −0.02, p = 0.034).
  • No significant between-group differences were observed for muscle hypertrophy, bilateral jump performance, sprint performance, or change-of-direction performance.
  • Subgroup analyses and meta-regression identified no significant moderators for unilateral jump performance or change-of-direction performance; sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness of main findings.

Supplements

Efficacy of Live Lactobacillus Preparations in Treating Cervical Inflammation and Clearing Viral Infections in Patients with HPV Infection Complicated by Bacterial Vaginosis

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Cui & Cheng · Afr J Reprod Health (2026-07-15) · n = 152 · retrospective analysis (described as randomized)

  • Retrospective analysis of 152 women with HPV infection + bacterial vaginosis (BV); observation group (n = 87) received standard antibacterial therapy plus Dingjunsheng (live Lactobacillus preparation); control group (n = 65) received standard antibacterial therapy alone; 6-month follow-up.
  • HPV clearance rate: 73.6% (observation) vs. 53.9% (control) — statistically significant difference reported but p-value not specified in abstract.
  • Cervical inflammation improvement rate: 90.8% vs. 76.9%; BV recurrence rate: 3.1% vs. 13.9%.
  • Dingjunsheng identified as an independent protective factor for HPV clearance in multivariate analysis: OR = 2.5 (95% CI 1.3–5.1).
  • Adverse reaction incidence was similar between groups; described as retrospective despite "randomly divided" language — methodology warrants scrutiny; single-center; sample imbalance between arms (87 vs. 65).

Examining the Feasibility and Preliminary Effects of Resistance Exercise Training and Creatine Supplementation in Individuals Treated for Colorectal Cancer

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Fairman et al. · PLoS One (2026-01-01) · n = 27 · RCT (pilot)

  • 10-week hybrid (in-clinic + virtual) resistance exercise training (RET) pilot RCT; 27 participants randomized to RET + creatine monohydrate 5 g/day (EXSUPP; n = 13) or RET + corn-starch maltodextrin placebo (EXPLA; n = 14); 3 sessions/week; all participants were post-chemotherapy colorectal cancer survivors.
  • Registry-based recruitment contacted 1,378 potentially eligible individuals; 410 were assessed; 27 enrolled (enrollment rate: 6.6%), indicating substantial recruitment challenges for this population.
  • Retention: 24/27 (88.9%); adherence to RET and supplementation: both >85%; serious adverse events: none reported.
  • No significant between-group differences observed for any secondary outcomes (body composition, muscular strength, physical function assessed via SPPB); both groups showed modest improvements in strength and SPPB scores from baseline.
  • Underpowered pilot design; baseline-adjusted ANCOVA models used; authors conclude a fully powered RCT is warranted; NCT06420726.

Acute Cordyceps militaris Supplementation and Elevated Resting Oxygen Uptake with Faster Reaction Times: A Randomized Crossover Trial

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Farzan & Koushkie Jahromi · PLoS One (2026-01-01) · n = 12 · randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover RCT

  • Double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design; 7-day washout; 12 recreationally active young men (mean age 21.6 ± 2.9 years; VO₂max 38.6 ± 5.5 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹); 1 g standardized Cordyceps militaris (CM) extract or cornstarch placebo ingested 30 min before maximal cycling test to exhaustion.
  • Post-exercise Stroop reaction time: CM condition 963.92 ± 84.99 ms vs. placebo 1034.33 ± 84.16 ms (p = 0.042, d = 0.88); significant main effect of Condition (F(1,11) = 5.45, p = 0.040) and Time (F(1,11) = 16.37, p = 0.002); Condition × Time interaction non-significant (p = 0.337).
  • Resting VO₂: CM elevated vs. placebo (0.37 vs. 0.24 L/min; p = 0.022); peak exercise VO₂: no significant between-condition difference (p = 0.490).
  • Cognitive accuracy remained near-ceiling (>99%) in both conditions with no condition differences.
  • No significant condition effects for blood glucose, blood pressure, or heart rate (all p > 0.05); very small sample (n = 12), male-only, single acute dose — limits generalizability substantially.

Sleep & Recovery

Association Between Circadian Rhythm Disturbances and Cognitive Decline in the Elderly: A Systematic Review

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Lopes et al. · Neurol Sci (2026-07-16) · n = 5,731 · systematic review

  • Systematic review of 10 studies; 5,731 participants screened, 5,707 eligible for analysis; inclusion criterion: adults ≥60 years with self-reported or objectively measured sleep data and cognitive assessment; studies tracking MCI-to-Alzheimer's progression were retained.
  • Circadian exposures organized into three domains: (1) actigraphic rest-activity rhythm metrics (amplitude, robustness, interdaily stability, intradaily variability, acrophase); (2) sleep quality and architecture parameters (efficiency, fragmentation, latency, WASO, melatonin timing); (3) circadian-disrupting lifestyle exposures (rotating night shift work).
  • Across all three domains, circadian dysregulation was consistently associated with higher risk of MCI development or progression to dementia.
  • No quantitative pooling (meta-analysis) was performed; findings are narrative synthesis across heterogeneous study designs and measurement tools.
  • Authors flag implications for public health and call for further research; study excluded conditions other than MCI/dementia and did not include participants with unspecified mean ages.

Delayed School Start Times to Improve Sleep Duration and Mitigate Depression in Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Wang et al. · J Clin Sleep Med (2026-07-14) · meta-analysis of 6 studies

  • Systematic search across Medline, Google Scholar, and Science Direct; 6 studies included in final meta-analysis; 4 independent reviewers conducted risk-of-bias and quality assessments.
  • Sleep duration: pooled analysis showed delayed school start time (DSST) associated with an average increase of 69 minutes/night; SMD = 0.63 (moderate effect size); analyses demonstrated high inter-study heterogeneity.
  • Depressive symptoms: pooled SMD = −0.20 (small effect); based on 5 studies; also showed high heterogeneity.
  • Minimal evidence of publication bias and small-study effects for both sleep duration and depression outcomes per authors' assessment.
  • Small number of included studies (6 for sleep, 5 for depression); high heterogeneity limits precision of pooled estimates; authors call for larger studies with standardized outcome measures.

Effect of Dexmedetomidine Hydrochloride Nasal Spray on Anxiety and Sleep in Patients Undergoing Gynecological Laparoscopic Surgery

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Zhao et al. · Med Sci Monit (2026-07-15) · n = 80 · prospective single-blind RCT

  • Single-center, prospective, single-blind, parallel-group RCT; 80 patients undergoing elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery (June–December 2024); both groups received 50 μg dexmedetomidine hydrochloride nasal spray 30 min pre-anesthesia; intervention group (n = 40) received an additional 75 μg at 21:30 on the night of surgery; control group (n = 40) did not.
  • Total AIS score on the night of surgery: no significant between-group difference (p = 0.771); however, the dexmedetomidine group had a significantly higher proportion of patients without subjective sleep deterioration (25.0% vs. 5.0%; p = 0.012).
  • Objective sleep metrics (wristband-measured): dexmedetomidine group showed longer total sleep time (p = 0.030), higher proportion of deep sleep (p = 0.040), and fewer awakenings (p = 0.020).
  • Hospital stay: significantly shorter in the dexmedetomidine group (p < 0.001).
  • Baseline characteristics, postoperative pain, and PONV were comparable between groups (all p > 0.05); single-blind design (patients unblinded); single-center; results limited to this specific perioperative context.

Skin & Hair Health

Fractional Microneedle Radiofrequency for Photoaging and Atrophic Acne Scar: A Split-Face Clinical Observation of Two Devices

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Sun et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026-07-10) · n = 20 · split-face RCT

  • Split-face design; 20 participants; treatment side received Peninsula Timerevert Needle (fractional microneedle RF, FMR); control side received EndyMed (established FMR device); outcomes included melanin index (MI), erythema index (EI), stratum corneum hydration (SCH), sebum, and dermal density.
  • Dermal density increased gradually following FMR treatment and was statistically significantly higher at 3 months than at all other follow-up time points on both sides.
  • No statistically significant between-device differences for participant satisfaction score, physician global assessment (PGA), erythema score, erythema/edema duration, MI, EI, SCH, sebum, or dermal density at forehead, cheek, or chin at any follow-up point.
  • Treatment side (Peninsula Timerevert Needle) showed shorter procedure time and lower pain score vs. control side (EndyMed), but higher bleeding score.
  • Very small sample (n = 20); no untreated control arm; outcomes limited to a 3-month maximum follow-up; findings on between-device differences are primarily non-inferiority in nature.

Dual-Depth 25-Pin Insulated Microneedle Radiofrequency for Moderate-to-Severe Periorbital Wrinkles

⭐⭐⭐ · Bae et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026-07-17) · n = 32 · observational cohort

  • Observational study; 32 adults aged 30–60 years with baseline Fitzpatrick Wrinkle Assessment Scale (FWAS) scores ≥4; three microneedle RF sessions at 4-week intervals; dual-shot mechanism delivering energy sequentially to 1.5 mm and 1.0 mm depth within a single needle insertion; outcomes assessed at baseline, weeks 16 and 24.
  • FWAS score: decreased from 5.44 ± 0.80 at baseline to 3.38 ± 0.61 at week 24 (p < 0.001).
  • Quantitative imaging (Antera 3D) revealed reductions in average wrinkle depth, maximum wrinkle depth, and indentation index; Cutometer parameters R2, R5, R7 all increased throughout follow-up (all p < 0.001).
  • Patient satisfaction at week 24: 90.63% satisfied or very satisfied; mean procedure time 7.2 ± 1.4 min; mean pain score 3.3 ± 0.9 (10-point scale assumed); adverse events limited to transient mild erythema and edema; mean downtime 1.5 ± 0.5 days.
  • No control arm; single-center; no blinding of treating physicians; observational design precludes causal inference.

Microneedle Therapy System Combined with Glucose 10% for the Treatment of Enlarged Pores and Fine Wrinkles

⭐⭐⭐ · Yi et al. · Aesthetic Plast Surg (2026-07-15) · n = 40 · prospective single-arm observational study

  • Prospective single-arm observational study; 40 Korean patients (24 women, 16 men; aged 28–72 years); 3–4 MTS (microneedling) sessions at 4-week intervals; immediately post-procedure, glucose 10% + hyaluronic acid (Sihler G) applied topically; outcomes at 12–16 weeks.
  • At final follow-up (12–16 weeks): two independent physicians rated 73% of patients as "much improved" or "very much improved" on GAIS; patient-reported GAIS in the same categories: 80%.
  • Mean satisfaction scores (VAS): 2.3 at baseline → 8.5 at study completion.
  • Pore grading improvements observed in 78% of patients; WSRS (fine wrinkle) scores improved in 68% of patients.
  • No control arm; single-center; Asian (Korean) patient population only; analyses primarily descriptive given the absence of a comparator; results are preliminary and hypothesis-generating per the authors.
