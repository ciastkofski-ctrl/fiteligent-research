## Obesity & Metabolic Health

### [Physical activity for the management of obesity in adolescents aged 10 to 19 years](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD015987)

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Meneses-Echavez et al. · Cochrane Database Syst Rev (2026) · n = 1,508 · meta-analysis

- 30 RCTs from 15 countries, published 2012–2023 (median year 2017), involving participants aged 10–17 years; interventions ≥12 weeks of any physical activity modality vs no-intervention control or active comparator.
- Primary outcomes included body weight, BMI, BMI z-score, waist circumference, body fat percentage, fasting glucose, and insulin resistance; secondary outcomes included mental well-being, physical activity levels, blood pressure, and lipid biomarkers.
- Physical activity vs no intervention: body weight MD −1.88 kg (95% CI −3.34 to −0.42); certainty of evidence rated **low**; all 30 included studies assessed at **high risk of bias**.
- Clinical and statistical heterogeneity (I² values not fully quoted in available abstract), small-study effects, and imprecise estimates further limited interpretability of the evidence base.
- Important knowledge gaps: no included study enrolled adolescents with disabilities; limited reporting of contextual factors; insufficient data for planned subgroup analyses.

### [Gastroparesis induced by glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists: A systematic review of clinical features, diagnosis, management, and outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0354497)

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Olubodun et al. · PLoS One (2026) · systematic review

- PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review; databases searched from inception to October 2025 (PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science); eligible study types included case reports, case series, observational studies, and clinical trials.
- 12 case reports describing 13 patients met inclusion criteria; most patients were female with type 2 diabetes mellitus, though non-diabetic cases were also documented.
- Semaglutide was the most frequently implicated GLP-1RA, followed by liraglutide, dulaglutide, and exenatide; symptom onset ranged from hours to several months after treatment initiation, commonly following dose escalation or inappropriate re-initiation.
- Presentations ranged from acute to chronic; symptoms included nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, bloating, early satiety, and oral intolerance; gastric emptying scintigraphy confirmed delayed emptying in selected cases.
- Discontinuation of the causative GLP-1RA led to symptom resolution in **all 13 patients**; methodological quality assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist for case reports.

### [Oral nonpeptide GLP-1 receptor agonist VCT220 for obesity treatment: a randomized, double-blind, phase II trial](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-026-02859-2)

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Ji et al. · Signal Transduct Target Ther (2026) · RCT

- Multicenter, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial across 13 sites in China (NCT06569355); 250 adults aged 18–75 years with overweight (BMI 24–28 kg/m² plus ≥1 comorbidity) or obesity (BMI ≥28 kg/m²) randomised 3:1 to VCT220 or placebo for 16 weeks alongside lifestyle counselling.
- VCT220 arms: 80 mg, 120 mg, or 160 mg once daily with slow or fast titration (five active arms); primary endpoint: percentage change in body weight from baseline to week 16.
- Mean body weight change at week 16: −5.75% (80 mg) to −9.73% (160 mg fast titration) vs −1.61% placebo (all comparisons p < 0.001).
- Proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss: 55.4% (80 mg) to 90.3% (160 mg slow titration) vs 13.1% placebo; VCT220 also improved HbA1c, fasting insulin, and blood pressure (effect sizes not fully reported in abstract).
- Adverse events mainly mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal events, most common during titration phase, rarely leading to discontinuation; no novel safety signals reported; phase III evaluation recommended.

## Longevity

### [The hormetic landscape: from evolutionary origination to mechanistic pathways in therapeutics](https://doi.org/10.1080/15384101.2026.2716406)

⭐⭐⭐ · Verma et al. · Cell Cycle (2026) · review

- Narrative review covering hormesis—the biphasic dose-response phenomenon in which mild stress triggers adaptive defense responses that increase cellular resilience—across toxicology, metabolism, aging, and evolutionary biology.
- Mechanistic pathways reviewed include mitohormesis, autophagy, xenohormesis, and epigenetic remodeling, with emphasis on signaling nodes Nrf2-KEAP1, AMPK-SIRT1, NF-κB, and heat-shock response.
- Addresses evolutionary framing of hormesis as an anticipatory "predict-and-prepare" strategy; discusses stress-induced reversal of differentiation in basal metazoans as evidence for conserved plasticity programs.
- Identifies translational challenges: defining the hormetic zone, inter-individual variability, and "bioactivity cliffs" between beneficial low-dose and harmful high-dose exposures.
- Proposes integration of AI and machine learning for nonlinear modeling of precise hormetic dosing in aging, regenerative medicine, and precision therapeutics; no original data presented.

### [Targeted inhibition of the BDNF/AKT/mTOR pathway in the inferior colliculus ameliorates salicylate-induced tinnitus in rats](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.117102)

⭐⭐⭐ · Lin et al. · iScience (2026) · animal study

- Rodent model: sodium salicylate (SS) administered intraperitoneally to induce tinnitus-like behavior in rats; tinnitus assessed via decreased gap pre-pulse inhibition of the acoustic startle (GPIAS) ratios and elevated auditory brainstem response (ABR) thresholds.
- SS exposure upregulated BDNF/TrkB/AKT/mTOR phosphorylation in the inferior colliculus (IC), a midbrain auditory relay nucleus, correlating with tinnitus-like behavioral phenotype.
- Bilateral IC microinjection of BDNF siRNA suppressed BDNF/TrkB signaling and ameliorated tinnitus-like behavior without improving hearing thresholds; systemic rapamycin reduced mTOR phosphorylation and partially alleviated behavioral manifestations.
- Study provides first direct mechanistic evidence linking BDNF/AKT/mTOR upregulation in the IC to tinnitus behavior in this model; results do not generalize to chronic sensorineural hearing loss etiologies.
- Findings are preclinical only; sample sizes, exact effect magnitudes, and statistical parameters not reported in available abstract.

## Strength & Hypertrophy

### [Shared mechanisms of musculoskeletal dysfunction in type 2 diabetes mellitus: Insights into future therapeutic directions (Review)](https://doi.org/10.3892/ijmm.2026.5955)

⭐⭐⭐ · He et al. · Int J Mol Med (2026) · review

- Systematic narrative review of T2DM effects on four musculoskeletal tissues: skeletal muscle, tendon, tendon-bone interface (TBI), and bone; no original data.
- In skeletal muscle: impaired insulin signaling and mitochondrial dysfunction drive reduced protein synthesis, enhanced proteolysis, and muscle atrophy.
- In tendons: advanced glycation end-product (AGE) accumulation and microangiopathy disrupt collagen metabolism and biomechanical properties.
- At the TBI: persistent hyperglycemia induces fibrocartilage fibrosis, impaired angiogenesis, and disorganized stress transmission, weakening load-bearing capacity.
- In bone: osteoblast-osteoclast imbalance combined with microvascular dysfunction and metabolic acidosis results in reduced bone formation and increased fragility; shared pathological drivers across all tissues include chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, ECM remodeling, and impaired regenerative capacity.

### [Transcriptomic Analysis of Bone Marrow Adipose Tissue Reveals Enrichment in Lipid Transport in Response to Western Diet](https://doi.org/10.1210/endocr/bqag092)

⭐⭐⭐ · Widjaja et al. · Endocrinology (2026) · animal study

- 20-week-old Sprague-Dawley rats fed chow diet (12% kJ fat) vs 12-week Western diet intervention (42% kJ fat); bulk-RNA sequencing performed on bone marrow adipose tissue (BMAT), white adipose tissue (WAT), and brown adipose tissue (BAT).
- Western diet significantly increased body weight (p = 0.02), whole-body fat percentage (p = 0.009), and adipocyte size in humeral BMAT (p = 0.02) and gonadal WAT (p = 0.005) vs chow group.
- Gene-set enrichment analysis of chow-diet animals revealed over-representation of transcripts related to cellular proliferation and adipoprogenitor cell populations in BMAT, suggesting developmental immaturity relative to WAT and BAT.
- BMAT exhibited a more robust transcriptomic response to Western diet than WAT; BMAT enrichment concentrated in lipid transport pathways, whereas WAT enrichment was more related to lipid biosynthesis and modification.
- Findings are preclinical (rat model); direct extrapolation to human BMAT biology and clinical musculoskeletal outcomes requires further investigation.

## Supplements

### [Effects of Probiotic and Synbiotic Supplementation on Body Composition and Glucose Metabolism in Children and Adolescents with Overweight or Obesity: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18152539)

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Tao et al. · Nutrients (2026) · meta-analysis

- PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis; databases searched from inception to 5 May 2026 (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, CNKI); 16 RCTs included; risk of bias assessed with RoB 2; certainty of evidence via GRADE.
- Primary outcomes: BMI z-score (MD = −0.03, 95% CI −0.11 to 0.05, not significant), waist circumference (MD = +0.60 cm, 95% CI −1.57 to +2.77, not significant), body fat percentage (MD = −0.29 percentage points, 95% CI −1.64 to +1.06, not significant).
- BMI: modest statistically significant reduction observed (MD = −0.91 kg/m²; full CI not available in abstract); GRADE certainty **low**, limiting confidence in clinical implications.
- Glucose metabolism: no significant overall effect on fasting glucose, fasting insulin, or HOMA-IR; subgroup analysis suggested possible fasting glucose effect for synbiotics, but based on limited number of studies—interpreted with caution.
- Subgroup analyses by intervention type (probiotic vs synbiotic) performed; conclusion: current evidence does not support clear benefits for body composition or glucose metabolism outcomes in this population.

### [Effects of Acute Caffeine Ingestion on Morning Strength, Power and Repeated Sprint Measures in Males: A Standardized Approach](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18152541)

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Vickery et al. · Nutrients (2026) · RCT

- Crossover RCT; 17 recreationally active, low habitual caffeine-consumer males; three experimental trials (caffeine 300 mg [2.5–5.3 mg/kg], placebo pill, no-pill control) in a counterbalanced, double-blind design; three familiarization sessions preceded experimental visits; rigorous chronobiological standardization applied.
- Primary focus: repeated sprint performance (6 × 20 m sprints), physiological responses, strength, power, and mood under standardized morning conditions.
- Caffeine did not significantly improve repeated sprint performance vs placebo or no-pill conditions; trend toward faster 20 m sprint times observed (CAFF: 3.39 ± 0.19 s vs PLAC: 3.43 ± 0.18 s vs NoPill: 3.41 ± 0.19 s; p-value not reported as significant).
- Caffeine **reduced perceived exertion** (RPE) vs placebo and no-pill conditions; no clear ergogenic effect on sprint performance under these conditions.
- Authors conclude that in low habitual caffeine users, central perceptual effects of caffeine are insufficient to overcome physiological constraints limiting morning sprint performance; findings emphasize importance of chronobiological standardization in ergogenic aid research.

## Sleep & Recovery

### [Intermittent Theta Burst Stimulation Improves Sleep in Autism Spectrum Disorder by Reorganizing Overlapping Brain Networks With Neurochemical and Transcriptomic Signatures: A Randomized Controlled Trial](https://doi.org/10.1002/cns.71041)

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · Zhang et al. · CNS Neurosci Ther (2026) · RCT

- 70 patients with ASD and chronic insomnia randomly assigned to real or sham intermittent theta-burst stimulation (iTBS) targeting the left orbitofrontal cortex; one session daily for 8 weeks.
- Primary outcome: Insomnia Severity Index (ISI); resting-state fMRI collected at baseline and post-intervention; Shannon-entropy diversity coefficient calculated to characterize overlapping network architecture.
- Sleep improvement following real iTBS was accompanied by changes in overlapping network architecture across 13 cortical regions; exact ISI effect sizes not reported in available abstract.
- Network changes showed spatial correspondence with mGluR5 and GABA_A receptor density maps (JuSpace toolbox analysis); transcriptomic associations enriched for glutamatergic synaptic transmission and calcium-dependent signaling, with peak gene expression during adolescence.
- Study provides multiscale mechanistic evidence for iTBS-related sleep improvement in ASD; generalizability limited to ASD + chronic insomnia population; specific sham-controlled effect sizes for ISI not available in abstract.

### [Association of poor sleep quality with adverse glycaemic outcomes in Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes: a family-based cohort study](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-124032)

⭐⭐⭐ · Wu et al. · BMJ Open (2026) · prospective cohort

- Prospective cohort study; Taizhou Diabetes Family-based Cohort, Zhejiang Province, China; n = 1,421 adults with T2DM; baseline sleep quality assessed via Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI); follow-up fasting blood glucose measurements through 30 June 2025.
- Each 1-point increase in PSQI score associated with 0.096 mmol/L higher fasting blood glucose (95% CI 0.081 to 0.111) in fully adjusted model.
- Poor sleep quality (categorical) associated with 0.669 mmol/L higher fasting blood glucose (95% CI 0.547 to 0.791) vs good sleep quality.
- Binary glycaemic outcome: each 1-point PSQI increase associated with OR 1.10 (95% CI 1.08 to 1.12) for fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L; poor sleep quality associated with 1.61-fold higher odds (95% CI 1.37 to 1.91).
- Higher PSQI scores also associated with greater glycaemic variability and higher odds of membership in less favourable longitudinal glycaemic trajectory groups; observational design precludes causal inference; population: Chinese adults with established T2DM.

## Skin & Hair Health

### [Extraction process, structural features, and bioactivity validation of Dendrobium officinale polysaccharides](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carres.2026.110054)

⭐⭐⭐ · Wang & Cao · Carbohydr Res (2026) · in vitro/preclinical

- Optimization of extraction-purification process for *Dendrobium officinale* polysaccharides (DOPs); two homogeneous fractions (DOP-1 and DOP-2) isolated via DEAE-52 and Sephadex G-100 chromatography; structures characterized by FT-IR, methylation-GC/MS, NMR, and SEM.
- Optimized process increased crude polysaccharide yield from 30.34% to 35.03% and polysaccharide content from 31.23% to 49.34%.
- DOP-1 (Mn 989 Da, PDI 1.15): linear oligosaccharide composed of →4)-β-D-Manp (72.32%) and →4)-β-D-Glcp (23.78%) in alternating pattern. DOP-2 (Mn 2,970 Da, PDI 1.67): branched polysaccharide with →6)-α-D-Glcp backbone (58.06%) and branches at →2,6)-Manp and →4,6)-Glcp.
- Anti-photoaging effects assessed in human foreskin fibroblasts (HFF cells) via CCK-8 viability, SA-β-Gal senescence staining, and qPCR of TGF-β/Smad pathway genes; both DOP-1 and DOP-2 at 62.5 μg/mL protected HFF cells from UVA-induced photoaging.
- Both fractions upregulated TGF-β1, Smad3, Smad4, and Collagen I, and downregulated Smad7 and MMP-1; DOP-2 showed dose-dependent effects; findings are in vitro only—no animal or human data.

### [Use of a nanosecond Q-switched 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser for photoaging management in Asian skin: a retrospective study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-026-04973-7)

⭐⭐⭐ · Zappia et al. · Lasers Med Sci (2026) · retrospective study

- Retrospective study; 50 Asian patients with Fitzpatrick skin types III–IV and facial wrinkles; three monthly treatment sessions with Q-switched 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser; outcomes assessed via Fitzpatrick Wrinkle and Elastosis Scale (FWS), standardized photographs, pain Visual Analogue Scale, and adverse event monitoring.
- Mean FWS score decreased from 4.54 ± 1.55 at baseline to 3.42 ± 0.97 at three-month post-treatment follow-up; mean absolute reduction of 1.12 ± 0.75 points.
- 41/50 patients showed improvement of ≥1 FWS point; 13/50 improved by ≥2 FWS points; 9/50 remained stable; no clinical worsening in any patient.
- Tolerability: mean pain score 2.0 ± 0.78 (VAS); mild transient erythema in 4/50 patients; no blistering, scarring, burns, hypopigmentation, or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation recorded.
- Key limitations acknowledged by authors: retrospective design, no control group, absence of instrumental objective measurements (e.g., profilometry, optical coherence tomography); findings require confirmation in prospective controlled studies.
