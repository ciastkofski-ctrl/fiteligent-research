Obesity & Metabolic Health

Global prevalence of underweight and overweight/obesity among migrant workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n = 135,404 · meta-analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines and searched seven databases without language, regional, or publication-year restrictions. Thirty observational studies with a combined sample of 135,404 migrant workers (mean age 32.3 years, SD = 11.1) were included. BMI thresholds of <18.5 kg/m² (underweight) and ≥25 kg/m² (overweight/obesity) were applied. The pooled prevalence of underweight was 4.7% (95% CI: 3.1–6.9%) and of overweight/obesity was 43.7% (95% CI: 39.8–47.6%), indicating a simultaneous double burden of malnutrition within the same population. Underweight was more prevalent among internal (local) migrants and in lower-middle-income settings, particularly in South Asia. Overweight/obesity was more prevalent among international migrants, especially in high-income countries and in the Europe and Central Asia region. Moderator analyses identified female proportion, destination country or region, income level, and marital status as statistically significant moderators for both outcomes. The review was registered in INPLASY (202540030). Limitations acknowledged include the observational design of all included studies and heterogeneity in how migration categories were defined across studies.

Source: Nurrahma et al. J Glob Health (2026). DOI: 10.7189/jogh.16.04154

Autophagy and metabolic homeostasis: Exploration in obesity-related metabolic diseases (Review)

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · review

This narrative review summarises current understanding of autophagy — an evolutionarily conserved catabolic process by which autophagosomes sequester intracellular contents and deliver them to lysosomes for degradation — as it relates to obesity-associated metabolic conditions. The review covers autophagy's role in maintaining intracellular homeostasis, its dysregulation under conditions of nutrient excess, and molecular mechanisms through which such dysregulation contributes to type 2 diabetes, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and atherosclerosis. The authors describe nutrient-sensing pathways that modulate autophagy flux, including mTOR and AMPK signalling, and discuss how chronic energy surplus interferes with these pathways. The review also notes that under certain conditions, inhibition of autophagy can attenuate some pathological consequences of obesity. A section on pharmacological agents targeting the autophagy pathway is included. As a review, this article does not present original experimental data; all conclusions are synthesis of published literature, and no systematic search protocol or quality assessment of included studies is reported.

Source: Lu et al. Int J Mol Med (2026). DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2026.5861

Longevity

Beyond expectations: nocebo suggestion affects cognitive performance in older adults

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · RCT

This online randomised controlled trial examined whether verbal suggestions could induce placebo or nocebo effects on cognitive performance in healthy adults aged 65–80 without direct experimenter–participant interaction. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups — Control (neutral information), Placebo (positive information), or Nocebo (negative information) — regarding an inert acoustic intervention. Cognitive performance was assessed before (PRE) and after (POST) the intervention using an oddball task in both auditory and visual modalities, measuring objective accuracy and response-time metrics alongside subjective ratings of expectancy and perceived efficacy. The Nocebo group demonstrated worsened objective task performance at POST consistent with the negative verbal suggestion, despite the absence of a measurable negative shift in expectancy or perceived intervention efficacy. The Placebo group reported higher expectancy and perceived benefit but showed no objective performance improvement relative to controls. This dissociation between objective and subjective components distinguished placebo from nocebo effects in this perceptual and attentional context. The authors note that the online setting, absence of in-person interaction, and unknown sample size are limitations affecting generalisability, and that motivational and individual-difference variables were not fully controlled.

Source: Barbon et al. Psychol Res (2026). DOI: 10.1007/s00426-026-02296-4

Beyond Performance Status: Optimize Cancer Care in Older Adults

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · review

This educational review, published as part of the American Society of Clinical Oncology Education Book series, addresses the limitations of performance status (PS) tools — specifically the Karnofsky Performance Status and ECOG PS scales — when applied to older adults with cancer. The review notes that adults over 60 account for approximately 60% of new cancer diagnoses and 70% of cancer-related deaths, yet remain consistently under-represented in clinical trials. It reports that studies find 43%–69% of patients with good PS scores have deficits identified by Geriatric Assessment (GA) that independently predict treatment toxicity and survival outcomes. The review summarises evidence from five landmark RCTs — GAP70+, GAIN, INTEGERATE, COACH, and PERI-OP — characterised as providing level 1 evidence that GA-guided interventions reduced grade 3–5 treatment toxicities by 10%–20% without affecting survival, through two mechanisms: treatment modification and intensive supportive care. Screening tools reviewed include the CARG score, CRASH score, Geriatric 8, and VES-13, each reported to be completable in under 30 minutes. A proposed clinical algorithm for integrating GA findings into treatment decision-making is presented. As a review, this article does not report original primary data, and no systematic search or formal quality appraisal of cited studies is described.

Source: Lichtman et al. Am Soc Clin Oncol Educ Book (2026). DOI: 10.1200/EDBK-26-100055

Dpp and immune response pathway factors mediate paracrine induction of senescent cells in Drosophila

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This study investigated the paracrine recruitment of senescent cells (SCs) in the fruitfly Drosophila melanogaster, using it as a model to examine mechanisms of senescence spreading. In mammalian systems, pre-existing SCs have been shown to induce senescence in neighbouring cells through paracrine signalling that requires Transforming Growth Factor β (TGF-β) activity. The present work examined whether an analogous phenomenon occurs in Drosophila, focusing on Decapentaplegic (Dpp), the fly orthologue of TGF-β/BMP, and components of the immune response pathway. The study characterised senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) elements — including cytokines, chemokines, and metalloproteinases — in the fly context and assessed the requirement for Dpp and immune signalling factors in secondary SC induction. The findings indicate that paracrine induction of senescence is operative in Drosophila tissue and involves these pathway components. Specific quantitative outcomes, effect sizes, and p-values were not available from the provided abstract. Limitations include the inherent translational gap between invertebrate and mammalian senescence biology, and the study design and sample characteristics were not described in the available abstract.

Source: Garcia-Arias et al. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2026). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2602167123

Strength & Hypertrophy

Creatine monohydrate for lean mass, strength, and bone density in postmenopausal women: a systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n not fully reported (7 RCTs) · meta-analysis

This pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis searched MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, SPORTDiscus, and Cochrane CENTRAL from 2000 to August 2025 for randomised, placebo-controlled trials examining creatine monohydrate supplementation in postmenopausal women aged ≥40–45 years with intervention durations ≥6 weeks. Seven RCTs were included. Outcomes included DXA-derived lean mass, one-repetition maximum (1RM) strength, bone mineral density (BMD), physical function, and safety. Random-effects meta-analysis employed the Paule-Mandel estimator for τ² with Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman adjustment. Subgroup analyses stratified by resistance training (RT) status, and exploratory dose/duration meta-regression, heterogeneity statistics (τ², I²), 95% prediction intervals, and GRADE certainty ratings were reported. Creatine supplementation at doses ≥5 g/day, particularly when combined with RT, produced small but reportedly meaningful increases in lean mass and strength. No safety concerns emerged across included trials. Effects on BMD were inconclusive. Total sample sizes across the seven included RCTs were not specified in the available abstract. The review was not prospectively registered; de-identified data were deposited on OSF post-analysis (DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/BVTRZ). Effect sizes, p-values, and confidence intervals for individual outcomes were not reported in the available abstract.

Source: Naddafha et al. J Int Soc Sports Nutr (2026). DOI: 10.1080/15502783.2026.2668435

BMP4 derived from human gastrointestinal carcinoma cells impairs myogenic differentiation: A possible in vitro mechanism of cancer-induced cachexia

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This in vitro study investigated the mechanisms by which human gastrointestinal cancer (GIC) cell lines induce skeletal muscle wasting, focusing on impaired myoblast differentiation rather than protein degradation. Conditioned medium derived from four human GIC cell lines was applied to C2C12 murine myoblast cells. The conditioned medium inhibited C2C12 myogenic differentiation through induction of Inhibitor of DNA Binding (Id) proteins Id1 and Id3, mediated via BMP–Smad1/5/8 signalling. This signalling axis suppressed expression of myogenin — a myogenic regulatory factor (MRF) — and downstream myogenic genes, resulting in failed differentiation into myotubes. Quantitative analysis identified elevated BMP4 secretion from all four human GIC cell lines. Pharmacological inhibition of BMP receptor signalling using dorsomorphin, or siRNA-mediated knockdown of BMP4 in GIC cells, restored myogenic differentiation capacity in co-cultured C2C12 cells. The study characterises BMP4 from human GIC cells as an exogenous inhibitor of myoblast differentiation operating through the Smad1/5/8–Id signalling axis. Limitations include the exclusive use of in vitro models, murine C2C12 cells as the myoblast system, and the absence of in vivo validation; translational relevance to clinical cancer cachexia requires further study.

Source: Higure et al. Int J Oncol (2026). DOI: 10.3892/ijo.2026.5891

Emerging role of DYRK1A as a target in cardiovascular diseases (Review)

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · review

This narrative review characterises the context-dependent roles of Dual-specificity tyrosine phosphorylation-regulated kinase 1A (DYRK1A) across three cardiovascular disease (CVD) categories: myocardial infarction (MI), cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). In MI, DYRK1A is described as inhibiting cardiomyocyte proliferation by suppressing cell cycle signalling — including the retinoblastoma protein/E2f pathway — and modulating epigenetic mechanisms, thereby impairing cardiac repair. In the context of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, DYRK1A is reported to inhibit abnormal myocardial growth by directly phosphorylating and antagonising Nuclear Factor of Activated T cells (NFAT), a pro-hypertrophic transcription factor. In PAH, DYRK1A is described as promoting pathological vascular remodelling through the STAT3/Pim-1/NFAT pathway inducing smooth muscle cell hyperproliferation, and through the DYRK1A/PPARγ/EGR1 pathway impairing endothelial function. The review highlights that DYRK1A exerts protective effects in some CVD contexts and pathogenic effects in others, depending on disease type and stage. This article does not present original data; no systematic search methodology or formal study quality appraisal is reported. All conclusions reflect synthesis of published literature.

Source: Yu et al. Mol Med Rep (2026). DOI: 10.3892/mmr.2026.13903

Impact of leucine-rich diet on placenta's proteomic and metabolomic profiles of pregnant tumor-bearing rats

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This exploratory, non-targeted study examined placental proteomic (mass spectrometry) and metabolomic (NMR spectrometry) profiles in a rat model of pregnancy complicated by cancer, with and without leucine supplementation. Four groups of pregnant rats were studied: control (PC), Walker-256 tumor-bearing (PW), leucine-supplemented (PL), and tumor-bearing with leucine-rich diet (PWL). Statistical comparisons were conducted on technical replicate data within each group. Tumor presence reduced maternal weight; leucine supplementation attenuated decreases in placental weight and fetal resorption rates. At the proteomic level, leucine restored expression of proteins involved in nuclear structure, cytoskeleton maintenance, and immune response that were disrupted by tumour presence. Metabolomic analysis revealed that tumour-bearing placentas exhibited decreased adenosine, glucose, glutamate, and succinate with elevated lactate, creatine, and histamine; leucine supplementation restored adenosine and pyridoxine levels and reduced lactate. Affected pathways — including alanine/aspartate/glutamate metabolism, purine metabolism, beta-alanine metabolism, and the citrate cycle — were modulated toward amino acid biosynthesis and pyruvate metabolism in the PWL group. Limitations include the animal model design, use of technical rather than biological replicates for statistical inference, and the exploratory non-targeted approach, all of which constrain direct clinical translation.

Source: Dos Santos et al. Cancer Biol Ther (2026). DOI: 10.1080/15384047.2026.2670792

Evaluation of the effects of probiotic supplementation on the ecology and inflammatory factors of female vaginal flora: a systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n = 15 studies · meta-analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, CNKI, and Wanfang from database inception to January 2026 for randomised and controlled studies evaluating probiotic supplementation in women. Fifteen studies were included. Outcomes assessed were vaginal pH, Nugent score (a standardised bacterial vaginosis diagnostic scale), and four inflammatory cytokines: IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α. Compared with controls, probiotic supplementation did not produce statistically significant reductions in IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, or TNF-α levels, nor did it significantly improve Nugent scores. However, probiotics significantly reduced vaginal pH (OR = 0.36, 95% CI: 0.15–0.89; p = 0.03), indicating maintenance of a weakly acidic vaginal environment. Sensitivity analyses showed generally stable findings, though substantial heterogeneity was noted across several outcomes. The authors conclude that current data are insufficient to support routine probiotic use for optimising vaginal microbiota composition or inflammatory status. The review calls for further high-quality RCTs. Specific dosing regimens, probiotic species or strains, and intervention durations varied across included studies and were not pooled separately in the available abstract.

Source: Xu et al. Afr J Reprod Health (2026). DOI: 10.29063/ajrh2026/v30i9.12

The 2024 Endocrine Society Guideline on Vitamin D: Comprehensive Summary and Critical Appraisal

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · review/guideline appraisal

This article provides a narrative summary and critical appraisal of the 2024 Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline on vitamin D, which was developed using GRADE and Evidence-to-Decision (EtD) frameworks, prioritising RCT evidence retrieved via systematic review. The guideline's primary focus was on vitamin D supplementation to reduce disease risk in individuals without established clinical indications. Based on RCT evidence, the guideline concluded that vitamin D supplementation reduces rickets and respiratory tract infections in children, reduces all-cause mortality in individuals aged ≥75 years, reduces pregnancy complications, and slows progression from prediabetes to diabetes mellitus. Accordingly, empiric vitamin D supplementation — defined as intake exceeding Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) without requiring prior 25(OH)D testing — was recommended for individuals aged 1–18 years, those aged ≥75 years, pregnant women, and individuals with prediabetes. The appraisal identifies several areas of criticism raised in the literature: unclear specification of recommended vitamin D dosages, limited applicability to populations not meeting inclusion criteria of reviewed trials, tension with prior vitamin D guidelines, and unresolved questions regarding the clinical role of 25(OH)D testing. The authors characterise the guideline methodology as rigorous but note it leaves open questions warranting further clarification.

Source: Pilz et al. Nutrients (2026). DOI: 10.3390/nu18091472

Sleep & Recovery

Normal polysomnography parameters in healthy children: a systematic review and meta-analysis

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n = 2,947 · meta-analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis searched Web of Science and Scopus for studies conducting overnight polysomnography (PSG) in healthy children, requiring scoring using American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) criteria from 2007 or 2012. Of 3,612 articles screened, 66 studies met eligibility criteria, yielding a pooled sample of 2,947 healthy children without previously diagnosed health conditions. Pooled estimates and 95% CIs for 34 PSG parameters were calculated using random-effects meta-analysis. Meta-regression assessed the influence of age and sex on each parameter. Age-related decreases were identified in total sleep time (TST), sleep period time, time in N3 and REM stages, number of sleep cycles, total arousals, total leg movements, periodic leg movement index, central apnoea index, apnoea-hypopnoea index (AHI), and mean peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO₂). Age-related increases were observed in other parameters (specific metrics not fully specified in the available abstract). Sex effects were assessed but specific findings were not detailed in the abstract. The study provides normative reference values intended for use in both clinical and research contexts for paediatric sleep assessment. Limitations include variability in PSG scoring between studies despite the AASM criteria restriction, and the cross-sectional nature of the contributing studies, which limits causal inference about developmental trajectories.

Source: Van Eyck et al. Eur Respir Rev (2026). DOI: 10.1183/16000617.0224-2025

The effect of preoperative melatonin patches on sleep quality in patients undergoing urological surgery

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n = 57 · RCT

This randomised controlled trial enrolled 57 patients hospitalised in the urology department of a university hospital in Istanbul, Turkey. Participants were randomly assigned to a melatonin group or a control group. Patients in the melatonin group received a transdermal melatonin patch containing 7 mg of melatonin at 11:00 PM in a quiet, darkened room on the night prior to surgery. Sleep quality was assessed using the Richards-Campbell Sleep Scale (RCSS); psychological well-being was measured using a visual analogue scale (VAS). Richards-Campbell Sleep Scale sleep-quality scores increased significantly in the melatonin group (p < 0.001), while the control group demonstrated lower sleep-quality scores. A statistically significant decrease in VAS well-being scores before and after application was observed in the melatonin group (p < 0.001), interpreted as improved well-being. Specific effect sizes, confidence intervals, and baseline comparability data were not reported in the available abstract. Limitations include the small sample size (n = 57), single-centre design, the specific context of pre-surgical anxiety which may limit generalisability to general insomnia, and the use of a single time-point assessment on the night before surgery.

Source: Toprak et al. Rev Assoc Med Bras (2026). DOI: 10.1590/1806-9282.20251486

Soft, skin-interfaced electronics enable cannula-free wireless monitoring of sleep respiration

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This engineering study describes the development and characterisation of a soft, skin-interfaced nasal patch designed for wireless, cannula-free monitoring of respiratory activity during sleep. The device is constructed from ultrathin elastomeric materials that conform to the nasal surface and couple respiratory-induced tissue deformation to a strain-sensing element. An integrated wireless platform transmits deformation signals to mobile devices without external tubing or tethered modules. Modular fabrication allows replacement of the strain sensor and skin-contact interface without compromising mechanical performance. Mechanical characterisation under physiologically relevant deformation demonstrated high repeatability and low hysteresis as reported by the authors. In vivo studies were conducted to compare the device's respiratory waveform capture against gold-standard nasal cannula measurements, with the abstract reporting close correlation between the two modalities; specific correlation coefficients and statistical parameters were not provided in the available abstract. The study does not report a clinical trial with defined participant numbers or formal inferential statistics on human subjects; characterisation methods were primarily bench-level and in vivo animal or limited human testing. The sample size and study population were not specified in the abstract.

Source: Lee et al. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2026). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2605960123

Skin & Hair Health

Sexual function outcomes of 5-year treatment with Rezum compared to doxazosin, finasteride, and combination drug therapy for men with benign prostatic hyperplasia

⭐⭐⭐⭐ · n = 1,243 (MTOPS: n = 1,157; Rezum: n = 86) · RCT cohort comparison

This study compared sexual function outcomes over five years between cohorts from two separate landmark RCTs: the NIH Medical Therapy of Prostatic Symptoms (MTOPS) trial (n = 1,157), in which participants were randomised to doxazosin, finasteride, combination therapy, or placebo; and the Rezum RCT (n = 86 sexually active men at baseline), in which participants received a single water vapour thermal therapy (WVTT) procedure. Cohorts were matched on baseline lower urinary tract symptom (LUTS) severity and prostate size. MTOPS participants completed the Brief Male Sexual Function Inventory; Rezum RCT participants completed the International Index of Erectile Function-15 (IIEF-15) and Male Sexual Health Questionnaire. Average percentage changes from baseline were modelled using a linear mixed repeated-measures model with fixed effects for treatment and follow-up visit. Men in the finasteride and combination therapy arms experienced significant worsening of sexual desire, erectile function, ejaculatory function, and overall sexual satisfaction. Doxazosin was associated with significant decline in sexual desire, erectile function, and ejaculatory function, though overall sexual satisfaction was preserved. WVTT patients experienced significant increases in sexual desire and erectile function at 1-year follow-up and significant improvement in overall sexual satisfaction over 5 years. Ejaculatory function decreased by approximately −10% from baseline at 3–5 years in the WVTT group, compared to approximately −20% for combination therapy. Primary limitations include the use of two different validated sexual function questionnaires across the two study populations, the non-equivalent sample sizes, and the observational nature of the cross-cohort comparison despite each component originating from an RCT.

Source: Han et al. J Sex Med (2026). DOI: 10.1093/jsxmed/qdag135

FEA-guided co-design of bubble microneedles: controlled tip separation for rapid transdermal and sublingual delivery

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This study presents a finite element analysis (FEA)-guided framework for optimising the geometry, cavity parameters, and matrix properties of separable bubble microneedles (BMNs) for transdermal and sublingual drug delivery. Two BMN formulations were developed: minoxidil sulfate-loaded (MXS-BMNs) for transdermal application and insulin-loaded (INS-BMNs) for sublingual delivery. FEA simulations predicted stress localisation at engineered cavities to reduce transverse separation force while preserving axial failure load. Measured transverse separation forces were 5.23 N and 4.17 N for MXS-BMNs and INS-BMNs respectively, compared to FEA predictions of 6.16 N and 4.79 N. Axial failure loads were 0.13 N and 0.28 N, reported as exceeding published penetration thresholds for skin and sublingual mucosa. Release studies showed more than 80% of payload liberated within 10 minutes. Ex vivo Franz diffusion cell testing showed MXS-BMNs achieved approximately sevenfold greater cumulative minoxidil permeation over 24 hours compared to an equivalent-dose topical gel. In vivo testing in C57BL/6 mice demonstrated accelerated hair regrowth with MXS-BMNs. INS-BMNs produced rapid and sustained glucose lowering in diabetic rats comparable to subcutaneous injection. The study is preclinical, with all in vivo evidence from rodent models; human pharmacokinetic and clinical efficacy data were not presented.

Source: Jiang et al. Drug Deliv (2026). DOI: 10.1080/10717544.2026.2667564

Protection of skin from UVB-induced photoaging: Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of avenanthramide C from oat sprout extract via suppression of MAPK pathways

⭐⭐⭐ · n not reported · study type not specified

This study examined the photoprotective properties of avenanthramide C (AVN C), a phenolic compound from oat sprout extract (Avena sativa), against UVB-induced photoaging in two in vitro systems: human keratinocyte cells (HaCaT cell line) and a 3D reconstructed human skin model (Neoderm-ED). AVN C exposure was associated with reduced oxidative stress through promotion of nuclear translocation of Nrf2 (Nuclear factor erythroid-2-related factor 2) and upregulation of antioxidant enzyme expression. It suppressed production of inflammatory mediators — COX-2, IL-1β, and TNF-α — via inhibition of NF-κB and upstream MAPK signalling. AVN C also inhibited UVB-induced expression of matrix metalloproteinases MMP-1 and MMP-3, which the study associates with prevention of extracellular matrix degradation. In the Neoderm-ED 3D model, AVN C protected against UVB-induced structural damage and inflammation, including suppression of prostaglandin E production (specific isoform and full data not available in the abstract excerpt). Quantitative data on dose-response relationships, specific effect magnitudes, and p-values were not available from the provided abstract. Limitations include the exclusive use of in vitro and ex vivo models, the absence of human clinical trial data, and the inherent constraints of cell-line-based systems in predicting in vivo skin responses.

Source: Ock et al. J Photochem Photobiol B (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2026.113458
